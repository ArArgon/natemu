### R3 — bounded re-entry record identity and ack semantics

- Lines 307–327 (the "Bounded re-entry policy (RC3, REV-190)" paragraph):
  two additions close the gap.
  1. Record identity: "That counter also fixes the re-entry's record
     identity: the sole permitted re-entry writes to a second, dedicated
     trace/ack slot keyed by the same worker/event/entry-depth plus a
     one-bit re-entry ordinal (0 for the original entry, 1 for the
     re-entry), never the first entry's slot, so the slot-uniqueness rule
     and the no-recycle-before-ack rule each hold per slot rather than
     colliding." This gives the re-entry its own slot (identified by the
     ordinal) instead of asking it to reuse or contend for the first entry's
     slot, so the "unique per worker/event/entry-depth" rule and the
     "not recycled until acknowledged" rule are each satisfied per slot.
  2. Ack semantics: "The re-entry does not republish a repair request; it
     re-observes the descriptor's already-published successful
     acknowledgement and, finding it still current, returns normally — the
     policy's effect is exactly one additional normal return, not a second
     wait on a fresh acknowledgement." This resolves the "again waits on the
     helper's acknowledgement" ambiguity R3 flagged, choosing the
     standing-acknowledgement reading (already implied by the paragraph's own
     precondition that the repair helper "has already published a successful
     acknowledgement") over a republished-request reading, and states the
     consequence explicitly so a code reviewer does not have to infer it.
- Closure: both elements R3 asked for — a defined re-entry record identity
  under the slot-uniqueness and no-recycle rules, and a settled,
  explicitly-stated ack semantics — are present.
