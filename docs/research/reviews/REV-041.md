## Verdict

- Verdict: `revise`
- Rationale: The scoping, boundary, and escalation discipline in this group is
  strong and should survive the revision. Every record separates what it
  measures from what it cannot, names its confounders concretely, reports full
  order statistics rather than a mean, routes each design question it provokes
  to a named backlog owner, disclaims selecting any architecture family, and —
  the part this review was asked to test hardest — bounds every `KA-01`,
  `KA-03`, `KA-04`, and `KA-07` statement and every family implication to this
  host, and every `KA-01` statement additionally to the ad-hoc signing
  configuration, with the Developer ID / hardened-runtime / notarized cell
  declared owed and pinned per run through the environment capture. `accept` is
  nevertheless prohibited, on eleven open actions. Three declared positive
  controls cannot fail for the reason the method gives them, and two of the
  three carry the only SMP evidence these records will produce: removing thread
  A's write acquisition from `INV-01-04` entirely leaves the cross-thread block
  and the checker verdict unchanged, `INV-01-05`'s per-thread attribution is
  satisfied by construction because no thread identity is recorded, and
  `INV-01-06`'s two "resume paths" preprocess to the same assignment. On the
  bitmap differential this review was asked to decide: declaring
  `overhead_resolvable: false` is not honest as written, because the record
  predeclares the null, the design guarantees it by measuring a cache-missing
  store against a sub-nanosecond bitmap update, and `CLM-01-05-13` nevertheless
  asserts a bitmap cost of "a few nanoseconds per store" that is the whole
  loop's cost — the measured differential is −0.050 ns/store against a declared
