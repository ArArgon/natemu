## Required changes

1. **`INV-01-11` owner, return to source gate.** `CLM-01-11-01` says QEMU as a
   whole is "GPL-2.0(-or-later, with a GPL-2.0-only carve-out for specific
   directories)". `qemu-license.txt` (SHA-256 `139c64b7…`, verified) says three
   separate things: ¶1 "The QEMU emulator as a whole is released under the GNU
   General Public License, version 2." — no "or later"; ¶2 "Source files with no
   licensing information are released under the GNU General Public License,
   version 2 or (at your option) any later version."; and the July-2013
   sentence, which governs which files *accept contributions* under version 2
   only, not the license of those directories. Restate the claim to track the
   three sentences separately. The record's own Confounders section sets this
   exact standard ("'GPL-2.0-or-later' versus 'GPL-2.0-only' … must be read
   precisely per dependency, not assumed uniform"); the headline claim does not
   meet it.

## Verdict

- Verdict: `revise`
- Rationale: This is careful source work and the integrity layer is complete —
  all twenty captures reproduce their ledger digests exactly, including the four
  reused from other Track 01 groups, and the great majority of cited passages
  re-extract verbatim from the captured bytes: QEMU's July-2013 directory list,
  the Linux syscall-note usage guide, libslirp's six named copyright holders in
  order, Capstone's three conditions, Cargo's SPDX-2.3 and
  `license`/`license-file` sentences, Arm's confidentiality metadata, Homebrew's
  four requirement bullets, and the `lldb(1)` license sentence. The record also
  does two hard things well: it resolves licensing *conditionally* against two
  named candidate distribution models rather than presuming an undecided
  answer, and it identifies a genuine, non-obvious envelope risk — the Intel
  SDM's implementation grant being scoped to software "intended to execute on
  the Intel product(s) referenced", which an arm64-hosted x86-64 emulator may
  fall outside — quotes it literally, and correctly declines to resolve it.
  `accept` is nonetheless prohibited. Three claims do not say what their cited
  text says: `CLM-01-11-01` adds "or later" to a sentence that says "version 2"
  flat, on the very axis the record's own confounder section says must be read
  precisely; `CLM-01-11-09` and the Intel row stop one sentence short of "No
  rights are granted to create modifications or derivatives of this document.",
  which bears directly on the excerpting an x86 emulator would actually do; and
  `CLM-01-11-10` drops two of the Virtio grant's stated conditions. Two rows
  break the record's own pinning method and one of them is a third-party mirror
  classified as upstream. One capture is hand-trimmed and therefore not
  reproducible from its origin. One `INV-01-10` claim carries a Source-backed
  label over an observation. One claim is a statement about the record rather
  than a proposition. And the coverage metric yields three different numbers
  against one table — 9 rows on recomputation, 13 in the metric, 14 in the
  Results narrative. Each change is small and checkable against a named line.
- Applicability limitations: This review adjudicates source support for
  `INV-01-10` and `INV-01-11` only, at revision `c497cf3`, against the capture
  store as of 2026-09-04. It establishes no toolchain fact, no `KA-09` or
  `KA-10` disposition, no family eligibility, and no legal conclusion — the
  subjects' own legal-uncertainty statement stands, and nothing here is legal
  advice or a substitute for the counsel `INV-01-11` says a family reaching
  planning would need. Digest verification is bounded by the capture dates and
  by the local SDK/CLT installation; the access re-tests reflect this network
  environment on 2026-09-04 only. It does not accept `INV-01-10`'s method
  (`REV-053`) or code (`REV-054`), and nothing here authorizes an evidence run,
  planning, or implementation.
