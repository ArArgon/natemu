## Disposition of `REV-222` R1–R6

**R1 — closed.** Line 388–390 now reads "its preflight-recorded SP
relationship to those parents must be strictly descending and non-overlapping
before the terminal entry is treated as valid". Lines 562–564 (unchanged, and
verified unchanged in the diff) declare blocking for "a non-descending or
overlapping SP sequence across depths". I checked the logic rather than the
wording: the blocking predicate is the disjunction (non-descending ∨
overlapping); its negation is (descending ∧ non-overlapping), which is exactly
what 388–390 now requires. The complement is exact under either reading of
"non-descending" (not-descending, or the mathematical non-decreasing) — both
negate to strictly descending. Line 574–577 (P depth-two) references "the
single-alternate-stack nesting check required above" without restating
polarity, so it was never the inverted party and inherits the one corrected
rule. The two mutually exclusive validity rules R1 identified are gone.

## Independent findings on the corrected text

*Independent citation sweep — one stale reference the correction introduced.*
The R3/R1 insertions shifted everything below line 307 by nine lines. The
author updated eight internal citations accordingly (508–517 twice, 377–380,
427–432, 551–569, 622–626, 777, 779–784, and the P/D range in

every internal line citation in the file and resolved each against the
corrected text. All eight updates are correct. One reference was missed: line
126–127, the per-claim falsifier bullet, still reads "Per claim, under the P/D
separation (lines 466–476)". The P/D separation paragraph now begins at line
475 and runs to 485 — the author's own updated value at line 103. As it stands,
466–476 points at the tail of the nested-terminal-witness paragraph plus the
first two lines of the intended target. This is the same citation-retrievability
discipline RC5 and R5 were about, in the one bullet `REV-222` verified as RC4's
closure, and it is a regression this return introduced. RR1.

*The SP fix is a real complement, not a rewording, and remains decidable
in-handler.* Beyond the logical check above, the corrected condition is still
executable on the handler surface at 236–246: comparing an entry SP (or the
handler's automatic-local address, the identity route at 255–258) against the
parent depth's value published in a lock-free atomic word is a load and an
integer comparison. `REV-222`'s caution stands unchanged and remains
non-blocking: depths run in different handler functions, so automatic-local
addresses are frame-offset proxies for SP; the strict-descent and non-overlap
test is safe at frame granularity.

*The stack-switch statement matches the capture's scope but strengthens its
direction.* The capture states a sufficient condition — if the process is not
on the stack, the system arranges a switch — plus the existence of the check.
Lines 555–556 and `CLM-01-19-07` state it as "only if", a necessary condition,
and the nesting consequence at 556–558 ("depth-two and depth-three entries do
not get a fresh stack") rests on that converse. I record this as non-blocking
for two reasons. First, `REV-222` R2 itself specified the "only if" form as the
correction to make, and the source gate is closed at `REV-155`/`REV-177`;
reopening the quantifier now would relitigate accepted text on a point no
prior reviewer raised. Second, and more important, the design is fail-safe
against the gap: if the platform instead re-armed a fresh stack per nested
entry, the depth SPs would not be strictly descending and 562–564 blocks the
affected P/D cells; if a nested entry ran on the regular stack, line 565's
"Handler entry/depth SP must fall in its own registered range" fails. Every
deviation from the derived behaviour blocks rather than silently passing, and
line 568–569 already refuses to convert the same-cell observation into a
per-thread contract. If the claim table is next touched, quoting the page's
"if … the system arranges a switch" alongside the "only if" restatement would
close the last gap between the capture and the derivation.

*SRC-090's capture is not digest-pinned inside INV-01-19.* Lines 74–89 pin the
reused captures by SHA-256 — SRC155, SRC072, SRC103, SRC084, SRC085/086 — and
say "The three text captures are under artifacts/…". `g2-man-sigaltstack.txt`
is cited at line 64 by filename and capture line range only, with no digest,
in a git-ignored tree. Non-blocking: the digest `c8eb2b10…` is pinned in the
shared source ledger at lines 272 and 406, and I recomputed it and confirmed
the match, so the pin is retrievable in the record system. Worth adding to the
paragraph whenever it is next edited, since R2 has made that capture's exact
wording load-bearing for the nesting design.

*The re-entry slot does not break slot uniqueness or the no-recycle rule, and
the standing ack cannot be stale.* Three separate checks:
(i) Uniqueness (line 421): the rule names the key worker/event/entry-depth;
the re-entry adds a fourth key component. Its purpose — no two records
occupying one slot, each slot separately acknowledged — is satisfied, because
315–318 says the re-entry never writes the first entry's slot and the slot is
"dedicated", i.e. reserved rather than drawn from a pool that could overflow
(419's overflow-is-integrity-failure rule is therefore not weakened). Recorded
as a non-blocking note: line 421 itself was not amended, so the four-part key
lives only at 316–318; naming the ordinal at 421 whenever that sentence is next
touched would leave one statement of the key rather than two.
(ii) No recycle before ack (250–251): with two distinct slots, the rule applies
per slot, which is what 318–319 asserts; no slot is reused before its own
acknowledgement, and nothing requires the first entry's slot to be recycled to
admit the re-entry.
(iii) Staleness: I checked whether "re-observes the descriptor's
already-published successful acknowledgement … finding it still current" could
be satisfied by an acknowledgement left over from an earlier event. It cannot.
Line 406 gives every witness "a fresh nonce and descriptor"; line 286 admits an
entry only when class, exact fault PC, address/range, operation, event nonce,
thread and live protocol phase all match that immutable descriptor; lines
303–305 restore PROT_NONE for the next event only after normal completion and
explicit disarming; and lines 324–326 score any re-entry after the descriptor's
witness token has been consumed as replay or failed repair. An acknowledgement
carried over from a previous event would belong to a different descriptor and
nonce and would fail the entry conjunction before the ack is ever consulted.
The phrase "still current" is therefore redundant rather than load-bearing,
which is the safe direction.

*The budget, the staging and the watchdogs are mutually consistent.* Lines
753–759 set a 5-second no-progress watchdog and a 30-second child ceiling. The
plan at 740–742 is 3 fresh process repetitions per positive cell, each with 10
warm-up plus 100 checked events: 110 events per child inside the 30 s ceiling,
about 270 ms per event, with the 5 s watchdog bounding each handler/helper
exchange. The aggregate bound is unaffected by per-exchange stalls in any case,
because the 30 s child ceiling dominates: no child can exceed it, so
children × 30 s is a true upper bound and the 8–42 h figure is conservative,
as 698–700 says. The R3 correction improves this rather than straining it: the
re-entry does not republish a repair request, so it adds no second helper wait
and no additional 5 s exposure — at most one extra fault and normal return per
affected event — and 687–689 queues any resulting rerun at the end of its own
stage. Negative-arm counts are stated (694–697, matching 743–744). Two
non-blocking wording notes: line 691 calls the multiplicand "composition
count" where 680–681 defines it as "fresh child processes"; and the first-stage
citation "lines 427–432" ends mid-sentence ("… and record" / "whether the
actual class repeats" at 433), though the prose exclusion immediately after
removes any ambiguity about what is in the stage.

*The first stage is genuinely Q18-free and now correctly scoped.* I re-verified
each named group against the corrected text: the no-JIT A and R baselines and
the no-JIT retry report (299–302), the mask/delivery/context-size preflight
(377–380), the D depth-2/3 non-JIT protected-page controls (427–432 with the
stated exclusion), the alternate-stack and no-`SA_ONSTACK` diagnostics
(551–569, now including the SP-relationship check), and the offline checker

publication/patch matrix at 508–517, and all remain behind method acceptance
