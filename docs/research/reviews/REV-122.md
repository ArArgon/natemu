## Required changes

- Required changes, owners, and return gate: none. Both `REV-110` required
  changes are closed and were verified by re-extracting the quoted figures
  from this reviewer's own raw output and by re-checking the sensitivity
  arithmetic.

**Answer to the question the record puts to this reviewer.** The author offers
to re-widen `I16` enforcement to the background arms and asks the return
reviewer to decide. **Keep the narrowing. Do not re-widen.** Four reasons, in
order of weight:

1. It costs no detection power. A timebase-ratio error is a property of
   `g_tb`, identical in every arm, so the enforced set is sufficient. I did not
   take this on the argument alone: in `REV-110` I injected a 1% artefact into
   the enforced arms only and `I16` failed as it should.
2. Re-widening reintroduces the failure mode that produced `MAN-01-07-01`. In
   my `--n 60` run the background same-thread arms already sat at 72% and 80%
   of tolerance (2 166 against 3 000 ns; 9 583 against 12 000 ns), and the
   metric is a worst case over N. A 2000-sample run is likely to exceed on at
   least one background arm, and the re-run would then produce no claim again
   for a reason that has nothing to do with the timebase.
3. The asymmetry of the two errors favours narrowing. A false PASS on a
   background arm costs nothing, because the same conversion artefact would
   fail the user-interactive arm at the same target; a false FAIL costs the
   entire run.
4. Nothing is hidden. The background deltas are still emitted per arm
   alongside their tolerances, so a genuine anomaly there stays visible.

One non-blocking recommendation attached to that answer, offered as guidance
rather than as a required change: at the result gate, the background arms'
reported deltas should be read even though they are not enforced, and a
background arm exceeding its tolerance while its matched user-interactive arm
sits far inside it should be recorded as a scheduler observation rather than
passed over in silence. That is what makes the narrowing safe rather than
merely convenient.
