## Resolution

Every guest image, instruction, descriptor, packet, and guest-originated I/O
request is untrusted. A conforming instance must:

1. contain fatal vCPU/translation/paging/interrupt/device/assertion failures so
   the supervisor, peers, and unrelated host apps continue;
2. access only explicitly granted host files, credentials, IPC, devices, and
   network endpoints;
3. confine guest memory and DMA-like accesses to declared bounds;
4. support routine target-product operation without root, an installed
   privileged helper, kernel extensions, private APIs, or undeclared
   entitlements;
5. enforce finite predeclared CPU, RSS, backing-store, descriptor, subprocess,
   and network budgets, rejecting/terminating only the offender;
6. mutate no host object outside the explicitly granted writable backing
   objects, and meet the predeclared snapshot/durability expectation within
   those objects after a crash; and
7. preserve supervisor recovery and peer progress—host panic/reboot/logout,
   supervisor/peer death, or unauthorized mutation is unconditional failure.

Later approved methods inject failures at execution/device boundaries,
malformed/cyclic descriptors, out-of-range addresses, simultaneous multi-vCPU
faults, allocation/file/FD/subprocess/CPU/network pressure, denied sentinel
resources, two live peers, and clean/fatal termination. Coverage includes each
named admissible host-generation/macOS pair and 2-, 4-, and 8-vCPU topologies,
P/E-core placement strata available to the accepted instrumentation,
oversubscription, and cross-vCPU pressure. Candidate public mechanisms,
including documented helper/service forms, may be studied without selecting or
implementing one. `Q-01-01` decides API admissibility, `Q-01-14` sets numeric
budgets, and `Q-02-04` owns the reviewed fault method.

### Operational oracle contract

These are minimum method requirements, not evidence that any mechanism passes.
`Q-02-04` may tighten values before execution; relaxing one reopens this scope
record and its adversarial gate.

| Property | Observable oracle | Observation cadence and timeout | Failure classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fatal-failure containment | Supervisor and two clean peer processes retain heartbeat and complete a signed sentinel transaction after offender exit. | Heartbeats sampled at most every 100 ms; peers must advance at least once per second and complete within 30 s after injection. | Supervisor/peer death, host logout/reboot/panic, missed progress window, or wrong sentinel result is containment failure. |
| Granted access only | A per-run manifest identifies canonical file objects, credential handles, IPC names, device handles, and endpoint tuples; allowed accesses succeed and paired denied sentinels remain byte/state identical. | Audit continuously during the case and compare before/after state within 30 s of termination. | Any unlisted access or denied-sentinel mutation/disclosure is access-control failure. |
| Memory/DMA bounds | Guarded host mappings and independent canaries surround every declared guest/DMA region; malformed and boundary requests exercise both sides. | Check canaries after each injection and at termination, with a 30 s case timeout. | Any out-of-bounds mutation/read disclosure, host signal, or silent acceptance is memory-containment failure. |
| Deployment boundary | A fresh standard-user account launches and completes the accepted case from a manifest of processes, services, entitlements, and loaded extensions. | Audit at launch and shutdown; normal case uses its workload timeout. | Root prompt/use, installed privileged helper, private API, undeclared entitlement, or kernel extension is boundary failure. |
| Resource budgets | `Q-01-14` supplies finite per-instance CPU, RSS, backing-store, FD, subprocess, and network caps; load crosses each cap while a clean peer runs. | Sample CPU/RSS at most every 100 ms and counters/events continuously; enforce or terminate within 1 s of the declared cap breach. | Cap not enforced, wrong process killed, peer budget theft/starvation, or unclassified global exhaustion is resource-containment failure. |
| Writable storage | Immutable hashes cover all non-writable host objects; writable backing objects use a declared snapshot/journal point and post-crash consistency/durability oracle. | Hash/state capture before injection and within 30 s after termination/recovery. | Mutation outside granted writable objects or failure of the declared in-object recovery oracle is storage-containment failure; intentional writes inside the grant are not by themselves failure. |
| Recovery and progress | Supervisor classifies offender termination, reclaims resources, starts a clean replacement, and both peers continue monotonic progress. | Progress sampled at most every 100 ms; replacement ready within the method's boot timeout, default `max(5 min, 10 × admitted-QEMU median)` fixed before data. | Unknown termination, leaked resources beyond 30 s, replacement timeout, or peer stall is recovery failure. |

Readiness is a signed in-guest marker emitted only after all expected vCPUs are
online and the workload oracle has initialized. Hangs are absence of the
required heartbeat/progress before timeout, never excluded timing samples.

## Falsifiers, controls, and limitations

- Falsifier dispositions: unauthorized host/peer effects or an uncontained host
  failure invalidate the property and all dependent results; a private-API or
  unavailable-entitlement dependency kills that family after `Q-01-01`; a
  privileged-service dependency reopens the deployment boundary and preserves
  alternative public families; a budget miss returns to `Q-01-14`; an oracle,
  instrumentation, or timeout ambiguity is a `Q-02-04` method failure; and a
  failure confined to one admissible host/OS/SMP cell narrows applicability or
  kills the family only after replication rather than proving project failure.
- Positive control: offender terminates while sentinel peer progresses.
  Negative controls deliberately target denied files/sockets and malformed
  descriptors.
- Metrics: peer/supervisor liveness and progress, unauthorized accesses or
  mutations, canary integrity, per-resource budget enforcement, recovery time,
  and termination classification; thresholds are zero unauthorized effects
  and 100% contained failures across the accepted matrix.
- No experiment/code/raw artifact/result exists here; method/code/result gates
  apply to the later fault investigation. Platform feasibility is a hypothesis.
- Newly surfaced questions: `Q-01-14`, `Q-02-04`; host-pair and placement
  coverage is assigned to existing `Q-05-02`/`Q-05-06`.
