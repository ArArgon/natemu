## Question and decision relevance

- Question: `Q-00-06` — Which concrete, checkable findings require a
  project-level stop, and which kill only one architecture family?
- Decisions affected: family eligibility, feasibility termination, bounded
  targeted research, and the synthesis choice among stop, continue targeted
  research, or authorize planning.
- Why now: termination rules must precede feasibility and performance results
  so that isolated failures do not kill the project and unfavorable evidence
  cannot be deferred indefinitely.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: a stop trigger must be an eligible reviewed finding;
  family and project scope are distinct; missing or invalid evidence cannot
  prove infeasibility; correctness failures invalidate dependent performance;
  economic break-even is benefit/cost ratio 1.0.
- Assumptions to challenge: one failed prototype represents every family;
  ordinary defects prove structural impossibility; a weak or noisy performance
  result is a kill; every uncertainty deserves another research cycle; missing
  required coverage can be ignored at synthesis.

### Family-level kills

Record a candidate kill for one registered architecture family only when an
eligible reviewed finding establishes one of the following, maps its exact
host/OS/guest/workload/SMP applicability, and shows that the failure prevents
the family from satisfying the complete required matrix. A separately
accepted universal mechanism may cover otherwise unmeasured strata:

1. The family requires a private API, unavailable entitlement, unsupported
   toolchain behavior, or prohibited distribution condition for a mandatory
   host/OS cell.
2. It cannot meet an accepted mandatory guest, direct-boot, device, SMP,
   isolation, or crash-containment requirement.
3. A structural correctness incompatibility remains after independent
   replication. An ordinary implementation defect is not structural
   impossibility.
4. Its dependency or license terms are incompatible with the accepted use,
   linkage, or distribution model.
5. After accepted Q-04-02 maturity, the accepted Q-05-07 procedure shows either
   the one-sided 95% upper bound on overall speedup below **1.25×**, or an upper
   bound below **0.909×** for a mandatory class/host/OS/guest/SMP stratum or
   mandatory cell that accepted Q-00-01 does not permit excluding.
6. After accepted maturity, a mandatory resource guard fails and an accepted
   Q-04-02/Q-00-07 finding establishes that the excess cannot be removed and
   makes the family operationally or economically unacceptable. A resource
   success-guard miss alone is not a family kill.
7. The accepted economic uncertainty procedure places its optimistic
   benefit/cost bound below **1.0** under the accepted Q-00-07 model.

### Performance-maturity prerequisite

Negative performance cannot kill a family until `Q-04-02` defines and passes
an independently reviewed maturity predicate covering mandatory paths,
correctness, release-build comparability, profile attribution, an exhaustive
registered optimization/falsifier list, optimization-search stopping, and all
evidence gates. The previously proposed values—90% host-CPU attribution and
two tuning rounds of at least five engineer-days with less than 5% aggregate
improvement—are test inputs, not predicates accepted here. `Q-04-02` must
evaluate at least attribution sensitivity of 80/90/95%, round budgets of
3/5/10 engineer-days, improvement cutoffs of 2/5/10%, and whether subsequent
independent profiling or tuning finds a decision-changing optimization. Until
that review accepts a rule, every negative performance result is immature and
cannot kill a family.

### Project-level stops

The following are candidate **technical/economic infeasibility inputs** to
Q-06-01, not project outcomes selected here:

1. A platform fact proves that every family in the closed register violates a mandatory
   public-API, entitlement, host/OS, or distribution constraint.
2. Every family in the closed register has a reviewed
   family-level kill.
3. No family in the closed register can meet the mandatory compatibility, SMP, correctness,
   isolation, or crash-containment boundary.
4. Every family in the closed register has an accepted economic optimistic
   benefit/cost bound below 1.0.
5. Every family in the closed register has an accepted mature performance kill;
   merely lacking a Q-00-04 pass is insufficient.

Items 2–5 require the closed finite register; one surviving un-killed family
falsifies them. A host-, OS-, guest-, workload-, or SMP-specific result has
only that applicability unless accepted evidence demonstrates a universal
mechanism.

### Governance cessation is not infeasibility

Missing coverage, an ungated or contradictory finding, an exhausted budget,
or absence of an accepted extension blocks `authorize planning`; it is not
technical or economic evidence against a family. At Q-06-01, the project may
still select the governance outcome `stop`—meaning cease research without a
feasibility conclusion—if the accepted research budget or authority for
further research is unavailable and no accepted Q-06-05 extension applies.
The synthesis rationale must label that
outcome `governance/evidence cessation`, list the unknowns, and must not claim
infeasibility. If an accepted bounded extension does apply, Q-06-01 may select
`continue targeted research`. No other record may select either outcome.

### Bounded targeted-research exception

The exact extension rule is owned by `Q-06-05` and requires independent review.
The candidate under test would require every item below:

1. one specific decision-changing unknown;
2. a falsifier and a method capable of resolving it;
3. an owner, review path, required equipment, and expected decision update;
4. a candidate maximum of 20 executor-days or 10% of remaining approved
   research budget, whichever is smaller;
5. no prior extension for the same failure mode; and
6. no already-satisfied project-stop condition.

The one-extension, 20-day, and 10% values are nonbinding hypotheses.
`Q-06-05` must test at least 10/20/40-day, 5/10/20%-budget, and one-versus-two
extension sensitivities; define recurrence by failure mode; and audit whether
the rule would have terminated a retrospectively decision-changing inquiry.
Until Q-06-05 is accepted, this exception cannot justify more research or a
stop. A scope revision remains a separately reviewed decision and cannot
silently alter a failed condition or denominator.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: after all named dependencies pass, the candidate rule kills
  demonstrably nonviable families without converting ordinary defects,
  missing evidence, or governance cessation into infeasibility.
- Falsifiers: a killed family later gains materially after satisfying the
  maturity predicate; an ordinary fix rebuts a purported structural failure;
  another admissible family survives a project stop; the 1.25×/1.50× zones
  conflict with accepted economics; a second extension is demonstrably
  necessary and bounded; or missing coverage is later obtained within the
  original budget.
- Predicted outcomes: isolated defects reopen evidence; conclusive failures kill
  only their family; technical/economic project-stop input occurs only on an
  envelope-wide failure or after every family in the closed register is killed.
