## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-08` — Can x86-64 SMP ordering, atomics,
  invalidation, and cross-vCPU events be represented on the supported hosts?
- Decision(s) affected: kill assumption `KA-06` (x86-TSO preservable on the Arm
  memory model with public instructions while at least two vCPUs run
  concurrently), jointly with `INV-01-12`; the SMP half of `KA-11`; the
  cross-modifying-code corner of `KA-07`; the ordering-strategy space screened
  by `Q-01-02` into families `F1`–`F4`; and the correctness evidence set of
  `Q-02-02`.
- Why this must be resolved now: SMP is a baseline, not an extension, so no
  uniprocessor result can support feasibility. If the guest's ordering and
  atomics cannot be represented on the host at all, the SMP boundary fails for
  every family and routes to project stop input 3. The answer also fixes what
  the `Q-01-12` prototype must compose and what `Q-02-02` must test.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the guest model is x86-TSO for ordinary accesses and
  locked read-modify-writes; the host is used through public instructions and
  public API only; a vCPU is a host thread for the purpose of this probe;
  correctness is a prerequisite for every timing sample, so no throughput
  number here is a performance result.
- Assumptions to challenge: that "x86 is TSO and Arm is weaker, so add
  barriers" is one statement rather than three separable ones (which x86
  reorderings are permitted, which Arm reorderings are permitted, and what the
  mapping costs); that guest atomics map one-to-one onto host atomics —
  alignment, granularity, and width all diverge; that a barrier "fixed" a
  litmus test when the relaxed outcome was never exposed in the first place;
  that non-observation over any finite iteration count establishes
  architectural prohibition; that a vCPU must be a host thread at all.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: the tested ordering gaps between x86-TSO and the Arm memory model
  are closable with public instructions while two threads run concurrently, by
  `LDAR`/`STLR` and by `DMB ISH` for both tested shapes. The
  atomics-granularity gap has a definite host behaviour and an affordable
  software fallback.
- Falsifier(s): an architecture-forbidden relaxed outcome remains observable
  in any strengthened arm — SB or MP under `LDAR`/`STLR`, or SB or MP under
  `DMB ISH` — which must be treated as a harness or method failure until
  resolved; a misaligned atomic outcome that cannot be reconciled with the
  applicable architectural conditions; a
  fallback cost that makes the case unimplementable rather than merely slow;
  or an invalidation handshake that does not complete. Cost alone is not a
  falsifier: an expensive but correct preservation strategy is a `Q-04-01`
  scaling input, and a negative performance result cannot kill a family before
  `Q-04-02`'s maturity predicate is accepted.
- Predicted outcomes: SB relaxed outcomes observed at a low but nonzero rate
  under plain accesses, and zero under both `LDAR`/`STLR` and `DMB ISH`;
  MP relaxed outcomes possibly zero even under plain accesses,
  because MP is much harder to expose under a lockstep barrier, and zero under
  release/acquire and `DMB ISH` — which is why MP is not a positive control;
  atomics crossing a 16-byte granule faulting with `SIGBUS` and

## Method

- Procedure and versions: (1) claim-level source audit of the Arm ARM issue
  M.c sections, the Intel SDM Vol 3A `253668-092US` sections, and the two
  peer-reviewed models, all captured on 2026-09-03 with digests; (2) a single
  C probe, `experiments/INV-01-08/src/litmus.c`, built with Apple clang
  21.0.0 against `MacOSX26.4.sdk`. Every access under test is inline assembly,
  and `make disasm` writes the disassembly so the code gate can confirm the
  emitted sequence is the one the arm claims rather than whatever the
  optimizer produced. In the evidence build `lit_body` inlines into
  `lit_thread`, where the C11 sense-reversing barrier also emits
  `LDAR`/`STLR`/`DMB` — so `make disasm` additionally builds an otherwise
  identical binary with `-DLITMUS_VERIFY`, in which `lit_body` stays a separate
  symbol containing the bodies and nothing else, and `validate.sh` checks that
  symbol's range. Without this a degenerated variant passes the instruction
  check unchanged, which `REV-045` demonstrated; the current check was
  re-verified against a deliberately degenerated build, which it rejects.
- Inputs and configuration: litmus at 10^6 iterations per arm across
  {SB, MP} × {plain `LDR`/`STR`, `LDAR`/`STLR`, `DMB ISH`} × {user-interactive,
  background}; the two threads are synchronized by a three-phase
  sense-reversing barrier per iteration so they actually overlap and so the
  scoring pass cannot race the peer's next iteration; the two shared locations
  are on separate 128-byte-aligned lines and the layout is fixed. Each arm
  records the full four-cell outcome histogram, not only the relaxed count, and
  side 1 takes a short pseudo-random start offset each iteration so the two
  sides do not always enter the body in lockstep (`BP-01-08-start`,
  "Measurement failure modes"; `REV-044` change 5). LSE arms:
  `ldadd` and `cas` on one counter at 2, 4, and 8 threads, 10^6 operations per
  thread, with the final counter value as an integrity check; each arm also
  counts *attempts*, so a `cas` retry is charged to the loop rather than to the
  instruction. Alignment arms:
  32-bit `LDADDAL` at an aligned offset (control), at a misaligned offset
  inside one 16-byte granule, across a 16-byte boundary, and across a 64-byte
  line, plus plain `LDR` and `LDAR` across a line, under a `SIGBUS`/`SIGSEGV`
  handler that refuses to swallow any fault raised outside a probe window.
  Fallback arm: four threads, one global mutex, one misaligned address.
  Round-trip arm: 20 000 epoch-and-acknowledge handshakes per QoS class.
  Environment capture as in `run.sh`, including `FEAT_LSE`/`FEAT_LSE2` and
  bracketing thermal state.
- **Boundary-straddling histogram pairs (amended after `REV-086`).** The
  adjudication under Alternative explanations compares both-observed fractions
  against an order-of-magnitude boundary. A ratio near that boundary can flip
  verdict on noise alone, and this is not hypothetical: `MP`/`DMB ISH`/
  background sat at 28.3× in `MAN-01-08-01` and 9.0× in the independent
  replication `MAN-01-08-03`, flipping INCONCLUSIVE → COMPARABLE between two
  runs of the same code on the same host. Predeclared handling, fixed here
  before any further run:
  1. A pair whose ratio falls within a factor of three of the boundary — that
     is, a ratio in [3.33×, 30×] — is reported **INDETERMINATE**, not
     COMPARABLE and not INCONCLUSIVE. An INDETERMINATE verdict licenses
     nothing: the strengthened arm's zero is neither corroboration nor
     dismissed, and the pair needs a second independent run before either
     verdict is used.
     **Zero-denominator case** (`REV-116` change 1): where the plain arm's
     both-observed count is exactly 0, the ratio is undefined. Such a pair is
     treated as an unbounded ratio and reported **INCONCLUSIVE**, never
     INDETERMINATE and never COMPARABLE — a plain arm that never got its two
     sides to observe each other cannot be compared with one that did, and no
     band around the boundary can rescue the comparison. This is not a corner
     case: `SB`/`LDAR`-`STLR`/user-interactive and `SB`/`DMB ISH`/
     user-interactive have a plain both-observed count of exactly 0 in both
     `MAN-01-08-01` and `MAN-01-08-03`, so two of the four pairs carrying this
     record's headline negative rest on this clause. The frozen checker's
     `max(lo, 1e-9)` guard happens to yield the same verdict, but the checker
     is not what the method text governs, and making the rule explicit in code
     is part of the code change owed after this amendment is reviewed.
  2. Where two or more runs of the same arm exist, the verdict is taken from
     the **pooled** both-observed counts across runs, not from either run
     alone and not from an average of ratios.
  3. The boundary itself is unchanged at 10×. This amendment adds a band
     around it; it does not move it, and it may not be moved after data
     exists without reopening this gate.

  **Rule 2 was applied to every pair, not only the straddling one**

## Controls

- Positive controls: the plain `LDR`/`STR` SB arm is the load-bearing control.
  If it never observes the relaxed outcome the harness cannot expose weak
  behaviour, and every relevant strengthened comparison is uninterpretable; the
  checker fails the run on that condition. The aligned `LDADDAL` arm is the
  positive control for the alignment probe, and the plain `LDR` at a
  line-crossing address is the contrast that shows an *ordinary* unaligned
  access is not what is being refused.
- Negative controls: `--negative` runs the two litmus sides sequentially on one
  thread. No interleaving is then possible and the relaxed outcome cannot
  appear — precisely the defect that would silently invalidate the whole
  litmus set. `src/check.py` must report `RESULT: FAIL` on rules `I2` (the
  positive control) and `I3` (concurrency) and exit non-zero; a checker that
  passes that run has not been validated. `--negative` reaches only part of the
  checker, so the fixtures under `experiments/INV-01-08/tests/` cover the rest;
  that experiment's `README.md` tabulates which fixture validates each of
  `I1`–`I14`, and `validate.sh` runs all of them and fails if any passes. The
  forbidden-outcome fixture is the one that matters most: it injects an SB
  relaxed outcome under `LDAR`/`STLR` into the valid fixture, which the checker
  must reject.
- Baselines: none. Capability evidence under the `Q-05-01` staging. No number
  here may enter a `Q-00-04` aggregate or a `Q-00-03` baseline comparison, and
  the LSE contention figures in particular are scaling observations, not
  throughput results.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: cache-line placement and false sharing, which change
  relaxed-outcome frequency; thread placement asymmetry, since P–P, P–E, and
  E–E pairings plausibly differ and QoS only samples them; the barrier itself,
  which imposes a lockstep that suppresses some interleavings (this is why MP
  is expected to be hard to expose); compiler transformation, which would make
  the probe measure the optimizer; frequency scaling and thermal drift on the
  contention arms; and, for the fallback arm, mutex implementation cost rather
  than the fallback design.
- Mitigations and residual risk: inline assembly plus a committed disassembly
  artifact addresses compiler transformation; fixed 128-byte alignment of the
  litmus locations fixes layout; the environment capture brackets the run.
  Residual risk: the barrier bounds which interleavings are reachable, so the
  observed relaxed-outcome *rate* is a property of this harness and must never
  be reported as a property of the hardware — only presence or absence is
  interpreted. Placement remains sampled, not controlled.

## Sample plan

- Units, repetitions, randomization/order, warm-up, and power/precision
  target: the unit is one litmus iteration, one atomic operation, one probed
  address, or one handshake. 10^6 iterations per litmus arm across 12 arms;
  10^6 operations per thread across 6 LSE arms; 6 alignment cases, each
  executed once because the outcome is categorical and a fault is
  deterministic given the address; 10^6 fallback operations; 20 000 handshakes
  per QoS class, sized so the reported p99 rests on at least 200 tail samples.
  Arm order is fixed, not randomized; side 1 of each litmus arm takes a short
  pseudo-random start offset per iteration, so entry offsets within an arm are
  varied even though arm order is not. No warm-up is discarded, which makes the
  contention figures conservative. One run per mode. The precision target for
  the litmus arms is presence-or-absence, not a rate. Without a justified
  independence/distribution model, the finite run supplies no model-based
  probability bound and establishes no prohibition.
- What the 10^6 litmus floor is for (`REV-044` change 6): it is a **recording
  convention with no inferential weight**, not a probability bound. It was
  chosen as roughly two orders of magnitude above the plain-SB relaxed-outcome
  counts seen in development smoke runs at 2 × 10^5 iterations, so that the
  positive control is very unlikely to read zero for want of iterations and so
  that arms are comparable to each other. It licenses nothing further: a zero
  at 10^6 is not stronger evidence of prohibition than a zero at 10^5, and no
  claim in this record may treat it as such. The architectural statements come
  from `SRC-105` and `SRC-108`, never from the count.

### Provisional resolution

`Q-01-08` is answered only in part, and only by the Source-backed and Inferred
claims above; the labels are explicit in the claim inventory and nothing here
rests on an unrun experiment.

Source-backed today: the guest and host ordering models are both specified.
Intel permits reads to be reordered with older writes to different locations
and forbids the other three listed directions (`CLM-01-08-01`). Arm defines
its own ordered relations and ordering mechanisms, including `DMB`,
Load-Acquire, and Store-Release; the cited Arm sections establish the identity
of those constructs but assert no one-to-one correspondence with any x86-TSO
litmus outcome (`CLM-01-08-02`). Where a cross-model statement is needed for
the release/acquire arms, it comes from `SRC-108`, whose axiomatic model
gives `bob ⊇ [L]; po; [A]` as one of the seven alternatives it unions into the barrier-ordered-before relation, and states that a
store-release before a load-acquire "is required to be observed in order by
all threads" — which is why the SB relaxed outcome under `STLR`/`LDAR` is in
the forbidden set of both this record and `check.py` rule `I5`. Atomics
granularity — Intel normally serves a locked access spanning multiple cache
lines with a bus lock, but an enumerated and enabled split-lock-disable
feature instead causes `#AC(0)` and prevents the access (`CLM-01-08-03`). Arm
faults an unaligned atomic when `SCTLR_ELx.A` is 1 and, with `FEAT_LSE2` and
`SCTLR_ELx.A` at 0, permits an unaligned atomic only within one
16-byte-aligned quantity, leaving the crossing case a `CONSTRAINED
UNPREDICTABLE` choice between an unaligned access and an Alignment fault
(`CLM-01-08-04`). Arm guarantees unaligned single-copy atomicity itself only
under the stated `FEAT_LSE2`, 16-byte-granule, and Write-Back Cacheable
Shareable conditions (`CLM-01-08-05`). Arm additionally specifies a
publication sequence for cross-modifying code (`CLM-01-08-06`). Inferred
today: the cited sources leave an ordering gap and an atomics-domain gap that
must be closed in software, and which mechanism closes either is a
family-screening question, not a platform fact (`CLM-01-08-07`). Both are gaps
in what the sources establish, not measured host behaviour.

What the run answered (`MAN-01-08-01`, reproduced by `MAN-01-08-03`). The
atomics half is measured, not projected. With `FEAT_LSE2` recorded as
implemented, the `CONSTRAINED UNPREDICTABLE` choice of `CLM-01-08-04` resolves
on this host to the Alignment-fault branch: a 32-bit `LDADDAL` crossing a
16-byte granule raises `SIGBUS`/`BUS_ADRALN`, one wholly inside a granule
completes, and a plain `LDR` at the same crossing address completes
(`CLM-01-08-10`). Non-atomic `LDAR` across a cache line faults the same way,
under a different clause (`CLM-01-08-19`). The software fallback that branch
requires costs 19.06 ns/op at four threads on one address (`CLM-01-08-11`).
The cross-thread handshake completed on all 20 000 attempts per QoS class,
user-interactive p99 167 ns (`CLM-01-08-13`). Neither LSE arm lost an update
at 2, 4, or 8 threads, and `cas` retry amplification separates cleanly from
instruction cost — 6.0069 attempts per success at 8 threads, 39.50 ns/attempt
against 237.25 ns/op (`CLM-01-08-12`, `CLM-01-08-18`).

Not answered — chiefly, **whether the host's public instructions close the
ordering gap in practice**, which is the question `Q-01-08` exists to ask.
Zero relaxed outcomes appeared in all four architecture-forbidden arms, but
under the frozen histogram adjudication all four SB pairs are INCONCLUSIVE:
the plain and strengthened arms sampled interleaving distributions differing
by three to six orders of magnitude, so alternative (b) — the harness ceasing
to expose weakness for reasons unrelated to the barrier — is not excluded and
those zeros corroborate nothing. Only the two MP `LDAR`/`STLR` pairs qualify
as corroboration, and only of the MP publication edge. The store-to-load
direction, which is the entirety of the x86-TSO-versus-Arm ordering
divergence, therefore has no experimental support in this record. Also
unanswered: mixed-size and overlapping accesses, never in scope; the
`SCTLR_ELx.nAA` value governing `CLM-01-08-19`, unreadable from EL0; and
behaviour on any other host or Arm feature level.

`KA-06` therefore has no disposition, and no disposition may be inferred from
any statement in this record about what the architecture forbids: a forbidden
outcome not observed is corroboration at best, never a `KA-06` pass, and in
the SB arms it is not even that. This resolution selects no preservation
strategy, ranks no alternative, and authorizes no design; strategy screening
is `Q-01-02`/`Q-03-01`, composition is `Q-01-12`, and the correctness evidence
set is `Q-02-02`.

## Alternative explanations

- Alternatives considered: (a) zero relaxed outcomes under a strengthened
  variant means the barrier worked; (b) it means the harness stopped exposing
  weakness for an unrelated reason, such as the added instruction changing
  timing enough to close the window; (c) an observed alignment fault is the
  architecture's `SCTLR_ELx.A = 1` branch rather than the `FEAT_LSE2`
  `CONSTRAINED UNPREDICTABLE` branch; (d) the LSE cost increase at 8 threads
  is contention rather than E-core placement.
- Evidence distinguishing them: (a) versus (b) is adjudicated by the outcome
  histogram, not by the relaxed count alone, and the reading is predeclared
  here (`REV-064` change 2). Each arm records all four `(r0, r1)`
  combinations, so the plain and strengthened arms of the same test can be
  compared on the *whole* distribution rather than on one cell. The diagnostic
  cell is the both-sides-observed outcome — `r0!=0,r1!=0` for SB, meaning both
  threads saw the peer's store — because it measures how often the two sides
  actually overlapped, independently of whether the relaxed outcome appeared.
  The comparison the result gate applies, fixed before data: for each test and
  QoS class, take the both-observed fraction in the plain arm and in the
  strengthened arm. If they are within an order of magnitude of each other,
  the two arms sampled comparable interleaving distributions, and a plain arm
  that observes the relaxed outcome while the strengthened arm does not is
  reported as corroboration of `CLM-01-08-02` — never as proof, since
  non-observation establishes no prohibition. If they differ sharply, the two
  arms did not sample comparable distributions, alternative (b) is not
  excluded, and **the comparison is reported as inconclusive rather than as
  corroboration**; the strengthened arm's zero then supports nothing, and the
  finding is that the harness cannot compare those arms on this host, which
  returns to the method gate rather than to a claim. This is not hypothetical:
  in the `REV-064` reviewer's reduced-count run the user-interactive SB arms
  recorded `r0!=0,r1!=0` in 0 of 50 000 iterations under plain `LDR`/`STR` and
  in 49 979 of 50 000 under `DMB ISH` — almost disjoint distributions, which
  under the rule above would make that pair inconclusive. (That run is not
  evidence: reduced count, under concurrent load, no manifest, and it supports
  no claim here.) The per-iteration start-offset jitter exists to narrow this
  gap, and whether it does is itself something the histogram will show. (c) is partly
  distinguished by the arm that is misaligned *within* a 16-byte granule: if
  that one completes while the crossing one faults, the `SCTLR_ELx.A = 1`
  explanation is excluded, because that branch would fault both. (d) is not
  distinguished, because placement is not controllable (`INV-01-07`
  `CLM-01-07-08`); the figure is reported as contention at a thread count and
  never as a core-type effect. Added at the `REV-044` return: (e) a `cas`
  cost increase at 8 threads is instruction scaling, versus (f) it is retry
  amplification in the CAS loop. These are distinguished by construction —
  the arm now counts attempts as well as successes, so `ns_per_attempt` and
  `ns_per_op` separate the two, and `attempts_per_success` is reported. A
  development smoke run showed the separation is real, which is why
  `CLM-01-08-12` and `CLM-01-08-18` are two claims with two thresholds.
- Applied to `MAN-01-08-01`: (a) versus (b) is **not** resolved in favour of
  (a) for SB. Under the rule frozen above, all four SB pairs are INCONCLUSIVE,
  so the barrier's apparent success there is indistinguishable from the
  harness having stopped exposing weakness; the two MP `LDAR`/`STLR` pairs are
  COMPARABLE and are the only ones whose zero is reported as corroboration.
  (c) is excluded on this run: the `ldaddal` misaligned *within* a 16-byte
  granule completed while the crossing case faulted, and `SCTLR_ELx.A = 1`
  would have faulted both — so the observed fault is the `FEAT_LSE2`
  `CONSTRAINED UNPREDICTABLE` branch, as `CLM-01-08-10` now records. (e)
  versus (f) is resolved by construction and by data: 6.01 attempts per
  success at 8 threads separates retry amplification (237.25 ns/op) from
  instruction cost (39.50 ns/attempt). (d) remains undistinguished, as
  predicted, because placement is not controllable.

## Limitations

- Scope, applicability, and uncertainty limits: one host, one OS build, one
  SDK, one Arm feature level; `P1` replication owed and unusually load-bearing
  because Arm feature availability is generation-dependent. Two litmus shapes
  only, no mixed-size or overlapping accesses, no IRIW/LB/RSW. Relaxed-outcome
  *rates* are properties of this harness, not of the hardware. Non-observation
  never establishes prohibition. No guest is involved, so nothing here shows
  that a guest's ordering is preserved end to end — that is `Q-01-12` for
  composition and `Q-02-02` for evidence. Oversubscription liveness is
  untested and owned by `Q-01-23`. Placement is sampled, not controlled. No
  number in this record is a performance result, a comparison, or a design
  decision, and no preservation strategy is selected here.
- Fixed sample counts: `--iters` scales the litmus arms only. The LSE arms
  (10^6 operations per thread), the fallback arm (250 000 per thread), the
  round-trip arm (20 000 handshakes per QoS class), and the six alignment cases
  have counts fixed in the source. A reduced-count development run therefore
  does not reduce them, and the "reduced iteration counts" this record refers
  to under Code validation apply to the litmus arms alone (`REV-045` change 8).
  The table is restated in `experiments/INV-01-08/README.md`.
- The 10^6 litmus floor is a recording convention with no inferential weight;
  see Sample plan. A zero at 10^6 is not stronger evidence than a zero at 10^5.
- Limits established by `MAN-01-08-01` rather than anticipated: the SB
  ordering comparison is **inconclusive on this host with this harness** in all
  four pairs, so this record supplies no experimental corroboration for the SB
  shape at all; only the two MP `LDAR`/`STLR` pairs qualify. Any downstream use
  must carry that, not the bare "zero relaxed outcomes" headline.
- **The plain-SB user-interactive positive control has demonstrably marginal
  power on this host.** Two full runs of the same code on the same host gave
  39 and **1** relaxed outcomes per 10^6 in that arm (`MAN-01-08-01`,
  `MAN-01-08-03`), against 1004 and 3299 at background QoS. `I2` is an
  any-arm rule, and that is the only reason the replication did not fail its
  own positive control on a single event. Consequences, which bind every
  downstream use: no SB conclusion may rest on the user-interactive stratum
  alone; a user-interactive SB result carries far less power than a background
  one; and the relaxed-outcome rate is unstable across runs even in order of
  magnitude — something this record asserted in principle under Confounders
  and can now show. A future run recording zero in that arm would be
  uninformative about the host rather than evidence about it.
- Every timing figure is a this-run value on one host under a shared load
  (1-minute load 1.61 before, 1.67 after). Magnitudes should be read as
  orders of magnitude rather than constants, on the general grounds that the
  host was shared and the arms were not repeated within a run. This record
  previously softened them by citing a "±30–50% run-to-run variance band
  reported on this host"; **that citation is withdrawn** — it named no
  manifest, review, or record, and the one comparison now available
  contradicts it for the metric it was used on (see the next item). The
  categorical outcomes — fault or no fault, relaxed outcome observed or not,
  updates lost or not — were reproduced exactly by `MAN-01-08-03` and are not
  subject to any such caveat.
- **The `T4b` miss stands plainly: 67.40× against a predeclared 60× bound**,
  and the independent replication measured 66.27× — reproducible to within
  1.7%. It is a miss, not a noisy near-pass, and this record no longer argues
  otherwise. It remains a `Q-04-01` scaling input rather than a `KA-06`
  falsifier, because cost alone cannot falsify `KA-06`; that is a routing
  statement, not a mitigation of the number.
- One host, one OS build, one SDK, one Arm feature level. `FEAT_LSE2` is
  recorded as implemented here; on a host without it, `CLM-01-08-05`'s
  conditional guarantee and `CLM-01-08-10`'s observed branch would both be
  different questions. `P1` replication is owed and is unusually load-bearing
  for exactly this reason.
