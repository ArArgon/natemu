  invalidation, and cross-vCPU events be represented on the supported hosts?
- Decision(s) affected: kill assumption `KA-06` (x86-TSO preservable on the Arm

- Why this must be resolved now: SMP is a baseline, not an extension, so no

  every family and routes to project stop input 3. The answer also fixes what
  the `Q-01-12` prototype must compose and what `Q-02-02` must test.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the guest model is x86-TSO for ordinary accesses and
  locked read-modify-writes; the host is used through public instructions and
  public API only; a vCPU is a host thread for the purpose of this probe;
  correctness is a prerequisite for every timing sample, so no throughput
  number here is a performance result.
- Assumptions to challenge: that "x86 is TSO and Arm is weaker, so add
  barriers" is one statement rather than three separable ones (which x86
  reorderings are permitted, which Arm reorderings are permitted, and what the
  mapping costs); that guest atomics map one-to-one onto host atomics —
  alignment, granularity, and width all diverge; that a barrier "fixed" a
  litmus test when the relaxed outcome was never exposed in the first place;
  that non-observation over any finite iteration count establishes
  architectural prohibition; that a vCPU must be a host thread at all.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: the tested ordering gaps between x86-TSO and the Arm memory model
  are closable with public instructions while two threads run concurrently, by
  `LDAR`/`STLR` and by `DMB ISH` for both tested shapes. The
  atomics-granularity gap has a definite host behaviour and an affordable
  software fallback. Affordability here is only the stated screening hypothesis;
  no workload-frequency model or complete guest fallback is established.
- Falsifier(s): an architecture-forbidden relaxed outcome remains observable
  in any strengthened arm — SB or MP under `LDAR`/`STLR`, or SB or MP under
  `DMB ISH` — which must be treated as a harness or method failure until
  resolved; a misaligned atomic outcome that cannot be reconciled with the
  applicable architectural conditions; a
  fallback cost that makes the case unimplementable rather than merely slow;
  or an invalidation handshake that does not complete. Cost alone is not a
  falsifier: an expensive but correct preservation strategy is a `Q-04-01`
  scaling input, and a negative performance result cannot kill a family before
  `Q-04-02`'s maturity predicate is accepted.
- Predicted outcomes: SB relaxed outcomes observed at a low but nonzero rate
  under plain accesses, and zero under both `LDAR`/`STLR` and `DMB ISH`;
  MP relaxed outcomes possibly zero even under plain accesses,
  because MP is much harder to expose under a lockstep barrier, and zero under
  release/acquire and `DMB ISH` — which is why MP is not a positive control;
  atomics crossing a 16-byte granule faulting with `SIGBUS` and
  `BUS_ADRALN`, per the implementation-defined branch of `CLM-01-08-04`; plain
  `LDR` at the same address completing.

## Method

- Procedure and versions: (1) claim-level source audit of the Arm ARM issue
  M.c sections, the Intel SDM Vol 3A `253668-092US` sections, and the two
  peer-reviewed models, all captured on 2026-09-03 with digests; (2) a single
  C probe, `experiments/INV-01-08/src/litmus.c`, built with Apple clang
  21.0.0 against `MacOSX26.4.sdk`. Every access under test is inline assembly,
  and `make disasm` writes the disassembly so the pre-execution review can confirm the
  emitted sequence is the one the arm claims rather than whatever the
  optimizer produced. In the evidence build `lit_body` inlines into
  `lit_thread`, where the C11 sense-reversing barrier also emits
  `LDAR`/`STLR`/`DMB` — so `make disasm` additionally builds an otherwise
  identical binary with `-DLITMUS_VERIFY`, in which `lit_body` stays a separate
  symbol containing the bodies and nothing else, and `validate.sh` checks that
  symbol's range. Without this a degenerated variant passes the instruction
  check unchanged, which `REV-045` demonstrated; the current check was
  re-verified against a deliberately degenerated build, which it rejects.
- Inputs and configuration: litmus at 10^6 iterations per arm across
  {SB, MP} × {plain `LDR`/`STR`, `LDAR`/`STLR`, `DMB ISH`} × {user-interactive,
  background}; the two threads are synchronized by a three-phase
  sense-reversing barrier per iteration to coordinate iteration phases so the
  scoring pass cannot race the peer's next iteration; it does not measure simultaneous execution; the two shared locations
  are on separate 128-byte-aligned lines and the layout is fixed. Each arm
  records the full four-cell outcome histogram, not only the relaxed count, and
  side 1 takes a short pseudo-random start offset each iteration so the two
  sides do not always enter the body in lockstep (`BP-01-08-start`,
  "Measurement failure modes"; `REV-044` change 5). LSE arms:
  `ldadd` and `cas` on one counter at 2, 4, and 8 threads, 10^6 operations per
  thread, with the final counter value as an integrity check; each arm also
  counts *attempts*, retaining distinct aggregate elapsed/attempt and
  elapsed/success denominators without isolating instruction latency. Alignment arms:
  32-bit `LDADDAL` at an aligned offset (control), at a misaligned offset

  line, plus plain `LDR` and `LDAR` across a line, under a `SIGBUS`/`SIGSEGV`
  handler that refuses to swallow any fault raised outside a probe window.
  Fallback arm: four threads, one global mutex, one misaligned address.

  bracketing thermal state.

## Controls

- Positive controls: the plain `LDR`/`STR` SB arm is the load-bearing control.
  If it never observes the relaxed outcome the harness cannot expose weak
  behaviour, and every relevant strengthened comparison is uninterpretable; the
  checker fails the run on that condition. The aligned `LDADDAL` arm is the
  positive control for the alignment probe, and the plain `LDR` at a
  line-crossing address is the contrast that shows an *ordinary* unaligned
  access is not what is being refused.
- Negative controls: `--negative` runs the two litmus sides sequentially on one
  thread. No interleaving is then possible and the relaxed outcome cannot
  appear — precisely the defect that would silently invalidate the whole
  litmus set. `src/check.py` must report `RESULT: FAIL` on rules `I2` (the
  positive control) and `I3` (concurrency) and exit non-zero; a checker that
  passes that run has not been validated. `--negative` reaches only part of the
  checker, so the fixtures under `experiments/INV-01-08/tests/` cover the rest;
  that experiment's `README.md` tabulates which fixture validates each of
  `I1`–`I14`, and `validate.sh` runs all of them and fails if any passes. The
  forbidden-outcome fixture is the one that matters most: it injects an SB
  relaxed outcome under `LDAR`/`STLR` into the valid fixture, which the checker
  must reject.
- Baselines: none. Capability evidence under the `Q-05-01` staging. No number
  here may enter a `Q-00-04` aggregate or a `Q-00-03` baseline comparison, and
  the LSE contention figures in particular are scaling observations, not
  throughput results.

#### Workload, lifetime and observed fields

- Use a fresh private writable allocation for the fallback, separate from
  alignment-probe side effects. Record allocation size and alignment, target
  offset 78, target width4 bytes, and its crossing of the 16-byte boundary at 80.
  Require the full target and two4-byte neighboring canaries to lie within
  the allocation; no wraparound/address overflow. Initialize target value 0
  using memcpy before any worker starts. Fixed canaries immediately before/
  after the target are 0xA5A5A5A5 and0x5A5A5A5A. Record their initial and final
  values. No other actor accesses the target/canaries while workers run.
- Four uniquely identified workers each attempt250000 increments. Set each
  worker explicitly to user-interactive QoS before ready, record/check each
  pthread_set_qos_class_self_np result, and fail before work on any error.
  No affinity or physical-core placement is claimed. Initialize
  one ordinary process-local mutex; record its initialization result. Every
  successful lock encloses the 4-byte memcpy load, uint32 increment and memcpy
  store, then unlock. The expected total1000000 fits uint32 without overflow.
  No unaligned typed C load/store is introduced. All accesses to the shared
  target participate in the same lock; this is the candidate's explicit
  compatibility boundary, not a proof for nonparticipating guest accesses.
- Coordinator owns per-worker argument/status storage until all created
  workers have joined. Each worker alone writes its own plain status slot;
  coordinator reads it only after successful join. Ready/go synchronization
  uses C11 atomics, not volatile publication. All4 workers acknowledge ready
  before go; this coordinates release, not physical overlap/core placement.
  No status storage, mutex or allocation is reused/freed while a worker can
  still access it.
- Record each create/join return, number created/joined, and each worker's
  ready/completed flags, requested/completed iterations, actual writes,
  successful lock/unlock counts and first error {operation, iteration, rc,
  injected}. Record mutex init/destroy results. A nonzero lock result must
  never enter the RMW body; a nonzero unlock result stops that worker.
  A create failure cannot leave a ready-count wait expecting an absent worker:
  a shared atomic abort flag releases ready/go waiters, and each worker checks
  it before entering the workload. No error arm is timed as successful work.
  Do not destroy/free after an unsuccessful join; terminate the child instead.
  Unknown lock ownership after an error is a failure, never a recover-and-time
  path. A blocking join/remaining worker is bounded by the external supervisor.
- After all successful joins, read target and canaries by memcpy and record
  initial_value, final_value, expected_value, expected_ops, completed_ops,
  actual_writes and lost_updates = expected_value-final_value using checked
  wide arithmetic. Normal acceptance requires each of4 worker rows to have
  requested/completed iterations=actual writes=successful locks=successful
  unlocks=250000, no errors, all readiness/completion flags, and all create/
  join/init/destroy/QoS results zero. Target must equal1000000, lost_updates0,
  and both canaries unchanged. Counts/status must agree with totals.
- Record timing endpoints as integer sender/coordinator Mach ticks plus
  timebase numer/denom, and elapsed_ns = floor((end_ticks-start_ticks)*numer/
  denom), computed with checked wide arithmetic (not separately rounded
  absolute endpoints). The checker independently recomputes this integer.
  The interval begins immediately before creating workers and ends after the
  last successful join, so it includes creation, readiness/go, scheduling,
  lock/memcpy/loop/status work and joining. It is not mutex-only or instruction
  latency. Initial allocation/setup and final checking are outside the interval.
  Require end_ticks>start_ticks, positive valid timebase, elapsed_ns>0 and
  positive exact counts before division. Derive T2 from exact elapsed_ns/
  completed_ops, not a trusted supplied decimal; preserve the strict500ns
  screen. If a display ns_per_op is emitted, validate it against the primitive
  ratio rounded to two decimals with exact nearest/ties-to-even arithmetic,
  never classify using it. The optional display must be finite and positive.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: cache-line placement and false sharing, which change
  relaxed-outcome frequency; thread placement asymmetry, since P–P, P–E, and
  E–E pairings plausibly differ and QoS only samples them; the barrier itself,
  which imposes a lockstep that suppresses some interleavings (this is why MP
  is expected to be hard to expose); compiler transformation, which would make
  the probe measure the optimizer; frequency scaling and thermal drift on the
  contention arms; and, for the fallback arm, mutex implementation cost rather
  than the fallback design.
- Mitigations and residual risk: inline assembly plus a committed disassembly
  artifact addresses compiler transformation; fixed 128-byte alignment of the
  litmus locations fixes layout; the environment capture brackets the run.
  Residual risk: the barrier bounds which interleavings are reachable, so the
  observed relaxed-outcome *rate* is a property of this harness and must never
  be reported as a property of the hardware — only presence or absence is
  interpreted. Placement remains sampled, not controlled.

## Sample plan

- Units, repetitions, randomization/order, warm-up, and power/precision
  target: the unit is one litmus iteration, one atomic operation, one probed
  address, or one handshake. 10^6 iterations per litmus arm across 12 arms;
  10^6 operations per thread across 6 LSE arms; 6 alignment cases, each
  executed once as categorical observations of those exact cases, without
  claiming all-address or future determinism; 10^6 fallback operations; 20 000 handshakes
  per QoS class, sized so the reported p99 rests on at least 200 tail samples.
  Arm order is fixed, not randomized; side 1 of each litmus arm takes a short
  pseudo-random start offset per iteration, so entry offsets within an arm are
  varied even though arm order is not. No warm-up is discarded; its net bias under uncontrolled scheduling/frequency

Source-backed today: the guest and host ordering models are both specified.
For the cited WB-cacheable scope and qualified ordinary-access cases, Intel
permits reads to pass older writes to different locations and forbids the
other three listed directions, subject to the named exceptions
(`CLM-01-08-01`); no unrestricted guest-memory-type contract is inferred. Arm defines
its own ordered relations and ordering mechanisms, including `DMB`,

of those constructs but assert no one-to-one correspondence with any x86-TSO
litmus outcome (`CLM-01-08-02`). Where a cross-model statement is needed for

## F08-WB1 — source-only access-discipline argument, 2026-09-05

Author: independent replication/source researcher `mach_baseline_replication`.
Status: **independently accepted conditional source argument and obligation map**,
after F08-WB1-R1 correction in the findings thread. No instrument changes, native

### Primary contracts and their boundaries

**Source-backed.** The retained Intel SDM Volume 3A, `253668-092US`, June 2026,
§§11.1.1–11.1.2 and 11.2.2–11.2.3 distinguish access atomicity from ordering.
Guaranteed ordinary widths depend on alignment/cache boundaries and instruction
form; the aligned 16-byte guarantee is limited to the listed forms on processors
enumerating AVX. Locked operations have stronger indivisibility guarantees.
§11.2.3's WB examples exclude several instruction/access classes, including
non-temporal accesses and paging updates. Most critically, §11.1.1 explicitly
allows completed component stores of some wider x87/SSE accesses to become
visible before another component faults. This defeats a universal architectural
guarantee of whole-instruction rollback; it does not require partial effects in
every such case or by itself invalidate an implementation that chooses an allowed
no-partial-effect behavior. §11.1 also leaves lock-starvation prevention to software.
See the [Intel manual publication page](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/intel-sdm.html)
and retained [revision text](../../../artifacts/research/track01/source-snapshots/2026-09-03/intel-sdm-vol3a.txt).

**Source-backed.** Arm DDI0487M.c §§B2.2.1/B2.8.2 condition single-copy atomicity
on access form, size, alignment and applicable features. Ordering clauses do not
turn arbitrary byte copies or overlapping accesses into one atomic operation.
§B2.2.5 separately restricts concurrent instruction modification and specifies
cache maintenance, completion and executing-PE synchronization requirements.
The restricted old-or-new instruction cases are not a general CMODX license.
See [Arm's architecture reference](https://developer.arm.com/documentation/ddi0487/mc/)
and the retained B2.2.1, B2.8.2, B2.2.5, B2.3 and B2.6 JSON captures in the
same source-snapshot directory; each `content` field decodes from base64 to HTML.

**Source-backed.** C11 N1570 §5.1.2.4p25 forbids data races; p26 explains the
interleaving interpretation for the stated race-free synchronization discipline.
§6.5p7 permits character-type object access. §7.26.4.3 specifies C mutex
synchronization. These clauses do not themselves establish a particular macOS
library implementation. A pthread-based candidate needs the public pthread
contract too: POSIX §4.15.2 specifies mutex acquisition/release synchronization
and ordering for a particular mutex. F08-2 exercises pthread availability and
checked calls on its recorded host; it does not establish every host's behavior.
See [N1570](https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg14/www/docs/n1570.pdf) and
[POSIX memory synchronization](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/basedefs/V1_chap04.html#tag_04_15_02).

**Source-backed model, narrower than the question.** Sewell et al.'s x86-TSO
operational model (§3.1) has per-thread write buffers, shared memory and a global
lock. Its introduction excludes exceptions, mixed-size/misaligned accesses,
non-temporal operations, self-modifying code and page-table changes. It therefore
cannot supply the missing whole-guest proof. See the
[authors' paper](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pes20/weakmemory/cacm.pdf).

### Explicit serialized candidate and conditional argument

**Candidate, not a design selection.** Represent guest physical WB RAM as owned
bytes, accessed only through one gate `G` with a checked public mutex contract.
Each vCPU may compute concurrently, but its memory operations enter `G` in guest
program order. The gate orders *guest memory events*, not necessarily whole
instructions. A nonlocked read-modify-write may have separate read and write
events. A locked instruction holds the gate across its architecturally required
read/modify/write and associated commit. No vCPU treats acquisition as successful
after a lock error. Initialization precedes publication; destruction follows join
or an independently established quiescence boundary.

All accesses to the same guest physical bytes must participate: ordinary and
locked CPU operations; mixed-width and overlapping operands; address aliases;
page-table walks and architecturally required A/D or descriptor updates;
instruction decoding; emulated DMA/device RAM accesses; debugger, snapshot and
invalidation helpers. This is a proposed invariant, not an assertion that Q12
already implements it. A physical device or kernel operation must not write the
owned RAM behind the gate. A possible adapter transfers through private buffers
and commits explicit device events under `G`; whether such events match a
particular guest device is a separate device-contract obligation. A mutex cannot
make an uncooperative physical DMA engine participate.

Access helpers operate on byte representations, with explicitly defined guest
endianness and local scratch values. They do not overlay overlapping C atomic
objects or dereference arbitrarily aligned typed pointers. For a single modeled
atomic guest event, all its bytes are read or committed while `G` is held. Thus
participating observers cannot see an intermediate byte copy, even if the host
implements that copy with several instructions. Mapping, rights and alias
resolution must remain stable over the event, under the same gate or a separately
proved compatible synchronization protocol. Host signals and other callbacks may
not re-enter the gate while its owner is interrupted.

**Inference, for the paper's fault-free ordinary/locked fragment only.** Linearize
each gated guest event in acquisition order. Per-vCPU program order is preserved;
each read observes the latest committed overlapping value in this order; a locked
RMW has no intervening participant. In the x86-TSO machine, choose each ordinary
store's propagation immediately after its enqueue, before scheduling another
event. Reads then find empty buffers; locked sequences run with the required
global lock and empty-buffer boundaries. This constructs an allowed strongly
ordered execution for that fragment. It neither reproduces every permitted weak
outcome nor gives an event-distribution guarantee. It supplies no theorem for
the paper's excluded classes or for guest liveness that depends on scheduling.

**Fault/commit boundary is unresolved outside explicit instruction contracts.**
For a scalar instruction with a source-established no-partial-store rule, an
implementation may validate the relevant translation/permissions before a single
commit and retain the correct guest exception state. That conditional statement
does not authorize applying the same rule to every scalar, wide, string or
multi-access instruction. Intel's component-store fault permission above leaves
the complete implementation argument unresolved: permission for partial effects
does not establish that they are mandatory, and cannot alone refute every
prevalidation/rollback implementation. For each admitted instruction and profile,
the chosen behavior must satisfy all applicable contracts, distinguishing permitted
effects from required effects. Fidelity to a selected concrete execution, if
claimed, needs separate evidence beyond an allowed-behavior argument. Required
component effects, fault priority, exception address,
restart state, paging side effects and interrupt boundaries need an instruction
and profile-specific event decomposition. Fault handling must release `G` before
dispatching or waiting; an unexpected host fault cannot be silently converted
into successful guest completion. Guest blocking operations must not wait for
another participant while retaining the gate. Those are witness obligations,
not established properties of an existing runtime.

### Comparison with a native atomic mapping

**Inference.** A native mapping could avoid the universal gate only after proving
its compatible discipline for each access class. Public acquire/release accesses,
barriers and LSE/exclusive operations must jointly preserve the guest ordering
relations, width and non-tearing guarantees, physical overlap, fault behavior and
compiler contract. Arm's conditional native guarantees cannot be inferred from
the guest opcode name or from one host's FEAT_LSE2 observation.

In particular, a mutex fallback on an awkward operand does not exclude ordinary
native loads/stores that ignore that mutex. Making those unrelated accesses C
`seq_cst` atomics would still not make a multi-access fallback one indivisible
transaction; mixing atomic and non-atomic overlapping objects also needs a valid
language representation. A range protocol, universal participation or another
explicitly proved interlock would be needed. This comparison names obligations;
it proves neither the impossibility of a native mapping nor its cost relative to
software serialization.
