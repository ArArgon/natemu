## Question and decision relevance

- Question: `Q-01-22` — Which host-slaved, rate-scaled, or virtual/execution-
  driven guest time-base policies are admissible, and what does each imply for
  timeouts, correctness oracles, and in-guest measurement?
- Decisions affected: timer/APIC contract, guest CPU/clocksource contract,
  correctness, benchmark eligibility, and `INV-01-02` family limits.
- Why now: host timer capability does not choose guest time semantics, and an
  implicit choice can invalidate guest boot calibration and all guest-reported
  performance values.

## Assumptions

- Declared: policy is assessed across P1/P2 and 2/4/8 vCPUs; no policy is a
  design choice during research; observed host facts remain cell-specific.
- Challenge: host monotonic time equals guest elapsed time; fixed rate is valid
  across sleep; execution-count time is compatible with external I/O; one
  clocksource policy suits correctness and benchmarking; late ticks may be
  dropped or replayed without guest consequences.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: at least one policy can give the accepted guest a coherent time
  contract in every admitted host lifecycle state, possibly while declaring
  some in-guest performance measurements invalid.
- Falsifiers: no policy simultaneously satisfies required guest monotonicity,
  cross-vCPU agreement, calibration, timeout, suspend/resume, and external-I/O
  semantics in an admitted product cell.
- Predicted outcomes: none before sources and dependency closure.

## Method

- Procedure: source and policy analysis. Enumerate guest requirements, candidate

  table; make no implementation selection.
- Inputs: exact guest/kernel/clocksource and CPUID/MSR contract; accepted

## Controls

- Positive: every policy must be tested on a known satisfiable toy contract and
  every source/host input must trace to an accepted claim.
- Negative: inject a backward clock, cross-vCPU skew, rate discontinuity,
  calibration disagreement, dropped deadline, and invalid guest measurement;
  each must make the relevant policy cell fail or become unknown.
- Baselines: host-monotonic-slaved, rate-scaled, virtual/execution-driven, and
  explicit pause-on-host-suspend policies; none is privileged.
