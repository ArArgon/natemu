## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-02` — Which emulator architecture families
  remain admissible after verified platform facts and project constraints?
- Decision(s) affected: the candidate set `Q-03-01` may compare; the
  denominator of every "all families" / "no family" quantifier
  `INV-00-06` permits (its dependency table makes `Q-01-02`/`Q-03-01`'s
  finite register a precondition for project-stop inputs 1–5); the routing of
  each `KA-` falsifier to a family-level kill or a project-level stop input;
  and the disposition order for a family that uniquely requires an installed
  privileged helper (`BP-00-end`, Architecture table, row 1).
- Why this must be resolved now: `INV-00-06`'s `CLM-00-06-05` forbids any
  project-wide disposition before a closed, independently reviewed family
  register exists, and its "Dependency and family registers" paragraph makes
  the register plus a breadth-search closure rationale the precondition. Every
  other Track 01 record cites the `F1`–`F6` labels and the `KA-01`–`KA-13`
  identifiers; without one record that owns them, each investigation would
  restate the register differently and no reviewer could audit the set as
  finite.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the accepted product envelope is unchanged — public
  macOS APIs only, unmodified modern x86-64 Linux guests with direct boot, SMP
  as baseline, headless device set (`INV-00-02` "Resolution"); routine
  operation without root, an installed privileged helper, kernel extensions,
  private APIs, or undeclared entitlements is a retained deployment boundary
  (`INV-00-08` "Resolution" item 4), not a selected process architecture
  (`INV-00-05` "Claim inventory and resolution"); the two minimum admissible
  host/OS pairs are `P1` (M1 Pro / Sequoia 15) and `P2` (M4 / Tahoe 26)
  (`INV-00-02` `CLM-00-02-10`); a family screen records admissibility, not
  preference, and `Q-03-01` owns comparison.
- Assumptions to challenge: that the six labels handed to Track 01 are
  exhaustive; that "expected killed by `KA-02`" is the same thing as killed;
  that a family whose distinguishing mechanism has no gated evidence is
  equivalent in standing to one whose mechanisms have been exercised; that the
  families are mutually exclusive; that a privileged-helper dependency has one
  obvious disposition; that a single host's results screen a family across the
  matrix; that vCPU-to-thread mapping, host page size, or device backing
  choice creates a distinct family.

### Breadth-search closure rationale

Closure is argued over the product of the three mechanism axes named in the
question, under the distinctness criterion of `CLM-01-02-07`. Sixteen
combinations exist; each is covered or excluded by a named reason.

The privilege axis collapses first. A **privileged helper** variant of any
combination requires exactly the platform surface of its unprivileged twin
plus an installation step that `INV-00-08` "Resolution" item 4 forbids for
routine target-product operation. It therefore introduces no new API or
entitlement (failing `CLM-01-02-07`'s first limb) and is separated from its
twin only by the disposition of `CLM-01-02-12` (satisfying neither limb as a
new *family*). The eight privileged-helper combinations are consequently
recorded as dispositioned variants of their unprivileged twins, not as
register entries. This is the disposition-order question below, not a
silent exclusion: if a surviving family were ever found to *uniquely* require
a helper, `CLM-01-02-12` says what happens to it.

The remaining eight combinations are the execution axis crossed with the
paging axis:

| Execution × paging | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| interpret × software MMU | `F1`. |
| interpret × hardware stage-1 in an HVF VM | Excluded as a variant of `F3`. An arm64 interpreter executing at EL1 under arm64 stage-1 tables that mirror the guest's x86 tables needs exactly `F3`'s platform surface — `com.apple.security.hypervisor`, VM and vCPU creation, EL1 execution, the granule premise of `CLM-01-02-10` — and is killed or spared by precisely `F3`'s falsifier set, differing only in the execution engine. Fails both limbs of `CLM-01-02-07`. |
| translate ahead × software MMU | `F4`. |
| translate ahead × hardware stage-1 in an HVF VM | Excluded as a combination of two registered families, not a seventh. `BP-01-02-start` ("Alternatives") already records that the families are not mutually exclusive and that a design may combine `F2`/`F4` translation with `F3`'s EL1 execution mode; such a design's platform surface is the union of `F3`'s and `F4`'s, and its falsifiers are the union of theirs. Screening the union adds no disposition the two entries do not already carry. |
| translate dynamically × software MMU | `F2`. |
| translate dynamically × hardware stage-1 in an HVF VM | `F3`. |
| execute natively × software MMU | Excluded as incoherent **and** independently killed. Incoherent: native execution means the CPU executes the guest's own instruction stream, in which case that CPU's own MMU performs address translation and a software MMU has nothing to translate. Independently killed: native execution of x86-64 on Apple Silicon is what `KA-02` denies, per `CLM-01-01-16`/`-17`. |
| execute natively × hardware stage-1 in an HVF VM | `F5`, killed conditionally (`CLM-01-02-09`). |

Four further candidates a reviewer might raise are dispositioned explicitly,
so the closure argument is not merely axis-complete:

1. **Virtualization.framework with a Linux guest.** Excluded by
   `CLM-01-02-06`: Apple documents the guest kernel as `aarch64` on Apple
   silicon Macs (`CLM-01-02-04`), so it cannot carry an unmodified x86-64
   Linux guest.
2. **Virtualization.framework plus the Rosetta directory share.** Excluded by
   `CLM-01-02-06`: Apple's documented scope is running Intel *binaries*
   inside such a guest (`CLM-01-02-05`), which is `F6`'s user-mode shape
   inside an `aarch64` guest kernel — outside the full-system envelope on two
   independent grounds.
3. **Kernel-extension- or private-API-assisted execution.** Excluded by
   `INV-00-05`'s non-goals (private APIs, kernel extensions, unavailable
   entitlements) and, if it ever reached evidence, by `INV-00-06` family-kill
   item 1. It is not admissible as a register entry under the accepted
   envelope.
4. **Remote or offloaded execution on other hardware.** Not an emulator
   architecture for this envelope; `BP-00-end` ("Required coverage", adjacent
   systems) already records remote execution as a zero-weight diagnostic
   control under `Q-00-03`, not a family.

Two dimensions that look like axes are not, under `CLM-01-02-07`: vCPU-to-host-thread
mapping (`CLM-01-02-13`) and device-backing choice (`KA-08`'s alternatives —
pty versus stdio for serial, BSD sockets versus a `vmnet` mode for network —
which vary within every family identically and are owned by `Q-01-09`).

This rationale is provisional. `CLM-01-02-14` states the register is not
closed; a newly credible family, or a contradiction in any cell above,
reopens closure and every dependent "all-family" conclusion.

### Resolution: privileged-helper disposition order

`BP-00-end` (Architecture table, row 1) records that nothing in Track 00
decides whether a dependency on an installed privileged helper is first a
reopenable scope boundary (`INV-00-05`, `INV-00-08`) or first a family kill
(`INV-00-06` family-kill item 1, "prohibited distribution condition").
`Q-01-02` owns the ordering. It is resolved here as `CLM-01-02-12`, an
**Inferred** claim:

> **The family-level kill applies first. Scope-boundary reopening applies
> second, and only if applying that kill would leave the closed register with
> no surviving family.**

The reasoning rests on four textual and structural points:

1. **`INV-00-05`'s reopening clause is already conditioned on the project
   level.** Its resolution says that "evidence that no admissible family can
   meet the boundary reopens this question rather than being discarded". The
   trigger is *no admissible family*, not *a family*. Reading the reopening as
   a per-family disposition would read that condition out of the accepted
   text.
2. **`INV-00-06` item 1 is the per-family rule, and it is cheap to apply.**
   `CLM-00-06-05` states that a family-specific failure does not stop the
   project while another family in the register remains un-killed. Killing one
   family that uniquely requires a helper therefore costs the project nothing
   while others survive, and it keeps the accepted envelope intact.
3. **The reverse order would widen scope silently.** If a helper dependency in
   any one family reopened the deployment boundary, then each family's
   convenience could relax an accepted product requirement — exactly what
   `CLM-00-05-02` forbids ("Promoting a non-goal requires a new gated scope
   question and reopens affected dependencies"). Under the order adopted here,
   the boundary is only reopened when keeping it would end the project, which
   is the situation `INV-00-05` actually describes.
4. **`INV-00-08` is consistent with this order, not against it.** Its
   falsifier disposition — "a privileged-service dependency reopens the
   deployment boundary and preserves alternative public families" — asserts
   the preservation of the alternatives, which is what the family kill
   delivers. It does not state that the reopening precedes the kill, and item
   4 of its resolution keeps "without … an installed privileged helper" as a
   conforming-instance requirement.

Three limits bound this ordering so it cannot be over-read:

- **It decides no actual family.** No registered family has been found to
  require a privileged helper; `KA-08`'s device backings and `KA-13`'s budget
  mechanisms are both being screened for unprivileged public forms.
- **Neither disposition may be applied before the fact is established.**
  `INV-00-05` requires `Q-01-01`/`Q-01-11` to establish that a family uniquely
  requires a prohibited mechanism before any kill; a family kill also requires
  an eligible reviewed finding mapping exact host/OS/guest/workload/SMP
  applicability (`INV-00-06`, "Family-level kills" preamble).
- **This record cannot execute the second step.** Scope reopening is owned by
  `Q-00-05` and requires a new gated scope question under `CLM-00-05-02`. This
  record states the order and routes the trigger; it does not reopen anything.
