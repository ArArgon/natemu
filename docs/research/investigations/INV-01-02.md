## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-02` — Which emulator architecture families
  remain admissible after verified platform facts and project constraints?
- Decision(s) affected: the candidate set `Q-03-01` may compare; the
  denominator of every "all families" / "no family" quantifier
  `INV-00-06` permits (its dependency table makes `Q-01-02`/`Q-03-01`'s
  finite register a precondition for project-stop inputs 1–5); the routing of
  each `KA-` falsifier to a family-level kill or a project-level stop input;
  and the disposition order for a family that uniquely requires an installed
  privileged helper (`BP-00-end`, Architecture table, row 1).
- Why this must be resolved now: `INV-00-06`'s `CLM-00-06-05` forbids any
  project-wide disposition before a closed, independently reviewed family
  register exists, and its "Dependency and family registers" paragraph makes
  the register plus a breadth-search closure rationale the precondition. Every
  other Track 01 record cites the `F1`–`F6` labels and the `KA-01`–`KA-13`
  identifiers; without one record that owns them, each investigation would
  restate the register differently and no reviewer could audit the set as
  finite.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the accepted product envelope is unchanged — public
  macOS APIs only, unmodified modern x86-64 Linux guests with direct boot, SMP
  as baseline, headless device set (`INV-00-02` "Resolution"); routine
  operation without root, an installed privileged helper, kernel extensions,
  private APIs, or undeclared entitlements is a retained deployment boundary
  (`INV-00-08` "Resolution" item 4), not a selected process architecture
  (`INV-00-05` "Claim inventory and resolution"); the two minimum admissible
  host/OS pairs are `P1` (M1 Pro / Sequoia 15) and `P2` (M4 / Tahoe 26)
  (`INV-00-02` `CLM-00-02-10`); a family screen records admissibility, not
  preference, and `Q-03-01` owns comparison.
- Assumptions to challenge: that the six labels handed to Track 01 are
  exhaustive; that "expected killed by `KA-02`" is the same thing as killed;
  that a family whose distinguishing mechanism has no gated evidence is
  equivalent in standing to one whose mechanisms have been exercised; that the
  families are mutually exclusive; that a privileged-helper dependency has one
  obvious disposition; that a single host's results screen a family across the
  matrix; that vCPU-to-thread mapping, host page size, or device backing
  choice creates a distinct family.

### Breadth-search closure rationale

Closure is argued over the three axes under the candidate/class distinction
in `CLM-01-02-07`. Sixteen cells exist; each retains a concrete disposition.

The eight installed-helper cells are outside `INV-00-08`'s retained routine
deployment boundary. This is a boundary exclusion of that cell, not an
assertion that its API surface equals its unprivileged twin, nor a kill of
every unprivileged realization of a candidate. Administrative reopening
order remains unresolved as `CLM-01-02-12` states; a gated scope change would
reopen this accounting.

The eight unprivileged execution/paging cells are:

| Execution × paging | Disposition |
| --- | --- |

| interpret × hardware stage-1 in an HVF VM | Retain `F3-I` within the `F3` umbrella as an untested interpretation variant. VM/EL1/paging composition is unestablished. No host MAP_JIT dependency or equal falsifier set to F3-D is assumed. |

Thus there are six retained unprivileged non-native cells (F1, F2, F4, F3-I,
F3-A, F3-D), two explicitly dispositioned native cells, and eight helper-boundary
cells. The six retained cells are not six independent classes: F2/F4 overlap,
and F3 subvariant dependency equivalence is not established. An eventual
all-family conclusion must cover each retained cell and its credible
dependency alternatives; it cannot use a six-label headcount, double-count
F2/F4 evidence, or kill F3's umbrella from failure of only one unproved
dependency. F6 is an additional out-of-envelope comparison label and is not
an admitted full-system cell.

Four further candidates a reviewer might raise are dispositioned explicitly,
so the closure argument is not merely axis-complete:

1. **Virtualization.framework with a Linux guest.** Excluded by
   `CLM-01-02-06`: Apple documents the guest kernel as `aarch64` on Apple
   silicon Macs (`CLM-01-02-04`), so it cannot carry an unmodified x86-64
   Linux guest.
2. **Virtualization.framework plus the Rosetta directory share.** Excluded by
   `CLM-01-02-06`: Apple's documented scope is Intel *apps and legacy Intel
   binaries* running inside an *ARM Linux VM* (`CLM-01-02-05`, `SRC-154`),
   which is `F6`'s user-mode shape inside an `aarch64` guest kernel — outside
   the full-system envelope on two independent grounds. A third, weaker
   ground is recorded without being relied on: the accelerated form of that
   path is documented as requiring TSO support "added to your Linux kernel",
   so even the guest it does serve is not an unmodified one.
3. **Kernel-extension- or private-API-assisted execution.** Excluded by
   `INV-00-05`'s non-goals (private APIs, kernel extensions, unavailable
   entitlements) and, if it ever reached evidence, by `INV-00-06` family-kill
   item 1. It is not admissible as a register entry under the accepted
   envelope.
4. **Remote or offloaded execution on other hardware.** Not an emulator
   architecture for this envelope; `BP-00-end` ("Required coverage", adjacent
   systems) already records remote execution as a zero-weight diagnostic
   control under `Q-00-03`, not a family.

Two dimensions that look like axes are not, under `CLM-01-02-07`: vCPU-to-host-thread
mapping (`CLM-01-02-13`) and device-backing choice (`KA-08`'s alternatives —
pty versus stdio for serial, BSD sockets versus a `vmnet` mode for network —
which vary within every family identically and are owned by `Q-01-09`).

This finite accounting rationale passed `REV-171` (`CLM-01-02-14`); it is
not proof of an absolutely exhaustive architecture space. A newly credible
family, or a contradiction in any cell above, reopens closure and every
dependent "all-family" conclusion.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: (1) **inherited conclusions** — citing another record's
  prose rather than its labelled claim would import an unearned confidence
  level; (2) **gate drift** — the cited records' gates are moving while this
  record is written, so a status recorded here can be stale within hours;
  (3) **argument from silence** — reading the absence of a restriction on
  `SRC-150` as a permission; (4) **axis choice** — a closure argument is only
  as complete as its axes, and the three axes were supplied to this record
  rather than derived by it; (5) **variant-versus-family judgment** —
  `CLM-01-02-07` is a construction rule, and a different rule would produce a
  different register size; (6) **asymmetric evidence between survivors** —
  `F2`'s mechanisms have been probed while `F3`'s have not, which can read as
  a preference if survival is stated without its basis.
- Mitigations and residual risk: (1) every cross-record citation names the
  `CLM` identifier, its evidence label, and its gate status, and quotes the
  owning record's own qualification where it exists; (2) the gate statuses are
  dated to the 2026-09-04 working tree and the "Results" section names exactly
  which cells must be revisited after the result gates; (3) `CLM-01-02-02` is
  stated as a negative-existence claim bounded to two captures and
  `CLM-01-02-03` is a Hypothesis, so no permission is inferred; (4) the axis
  set is stated explicitly and named as challengeable in the falsifiers;
  (5) `CLM-01-02-07` is written as a claim with its own derivation so it can be
  rejected on its merits; (6) `F3`'s row and `CLM-01-02-11` both state its
  thinner basis in the same sentence as its survival. Residual risk: an
  adversarial reviewer may reasonably conclude that an interpreter inside an
  HVF VM, or an ahead-of-time-plus-EL1 hybrid, deserves its own register entry;
  that would enlarge the register without changing any current disposition.
