## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-05` — Can guest page-table semantics and
  TLB-style invalidation be honored on the supported hosts, and which public
  mechanisms track modification of guest memory, including self-modifying code,
  and at what granularity and cost?
- Decision(s) affected: `KA-07` and therefore the admissibility of every family
  whose correctness depends on noticing guest writes (`F2`, `F4`, and any
  invalidate-on-write variant of `F1`); the invalidation cost component of
  `Q-04-01`; the correctness surface of `Q-02-01` and the SMP evidence of
  `Q-02-02`; the quiescence protocol of `Q-01-18`; and the prototype of
  `Q-01-12`.
- Why this must be resolved now: an unmodified guest writes its own page tables
  and may execute code it has just written. A translation cache that does not
  observe those writes produces wrong answers rather than crashes. The
  granularity available on the host (16 KiB) does not match the guest page size
  (4 KiB), so the mismatch must be characterized before any family is described
  as admissible.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the accepted guest uses four-level paging with 4 KiB
  and 2 MiB pages; SMP is baseline; guest memory lives in ordinary host
  mappings inside one process; the host has no per-thread page table, so a
  protection change is process-wide.
- Assumptions to challenge: that "TLB invalidation" is one problem rather than
  three (cached translations, dependent translated code, and cross-vCPU
  visibility); that host-page protection is the mechanism rather than one
  candidate among several; that a single-threaded fault cost describes an
  emulator with 2–8 vCPU threads; that `mprotect` cost is independent of how
  fragmented the VM map already is; that a sub-host-page protection request is
  merely inefficient rather than potentially incorrect; that a microbenchmark
  differential between two nearly identical loops measures the work rather than
  the order in which they ran.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: host protection faults provide sound modification tracking at
  16 KiB granularity with a per-fault cost of microseconds that degrades under
  concurrency; guest-page granularity is unreachable through host protection
  and must come from software checks; and the guest's architectural invalidation
  and shoot-down requirements are expressible because the host mapping is shared
  by all vCPU threads, which makes a protection change globally visible without
  a shootdown of its own.
- Falsifier(s): the handler does not receive an address that identifies the
  modified page; some protected writes produce no fault (unsound tracking); a
  sub-host-page protection request is silently rounded so a guest page's
  protection cannot be expressed independently of its neighbours; concurrent
  faults are lost or misattributed across threads; or the per-fault cost is so
  high that no plausible fault rate is affordable — the last of these being a
  cost finding for `Q-04-01`, not a kill at this track.
- Predicted outcomes: one fault per protected page written, all addresses
  inside the region; a few microseconds median single-threaded and a higher
  median with two threads; `EINVAL` on the sub-page request; `SIGBUS` versus
  `SIGSEGV` distinguishing protection violations from unmapped accesses; and an
  unresolvable software-bitmap differential with absolute per-store costs of a
  few nanoseconds.

## Method

- Procedure and versions: `experiments/INV-01-05/src/probe.c`, built by
  `Makefile` with Apple clang 21.0.0 at `-O1 -Wall -Wextra -std=c11` against
  SDK 26.4, driven by `run.sh`. A 64 MiB anonymous region is fully written once
  before measurement so first-touch commit faults are not charged to tracking.
  The probe then emits one JSON object covering: write tracking over 2000
  protected host pages under a `SIGSEGV`/`SIGBUS` handler that records
  `si_addr`, `mprotect`s the containing host page back to read/write, and
  returns so the store retries, with the round trip timed around the store;
  `mprotect` cost over 5000 alternating calls on one page; a two-thread case in
  which each thread faults over 500 pages of its own disjoint half of the
  region, released together by a shared gate, with per-thread attribution and
  timing; a granularity case comparing a 4 KiB-aligned non-host-page-aligned
  `mprotect` with a host-page-aligned control and then probing the writability
  of each 4 KiB slot under a trapping handler; a signal-class case comparing a
  write to a mapped read-only page with a write to an unmapped address; and a
  software dirty-bitmap comparison of 2^20 stores per repetition, 8
  repetitions, with two discarded warm-up passes and the two variants swapping
  positions on alternate repetitions.
- Inputs and configuration: no external inputs; region size, page counts,
  iteration counts, and repetition counts are compiled in and echoed in the
  output. `run.sh` enforces the `NATEMU_RAW_ROOT` rule and builds into the run
  directory. The environment capture is as in `INV-01-03`.
- Evidence labels assigned to planned claims: `CLM-01-05-07` through
  `CLM-01-05-14` are Hypothesis until the evidence run and its manifests exist;
  `-07` to `-13` then become Observed for this host and `-14` becomes Inferred
  over them and over `INV-01-06`.

## Controls

- Positive controls: one recorded fault per protected page written, with every
  recorded address inside the tracked region — a mechanism that cannot name the
  modified page is unsound regardless of its cost; a host-page-aligned
  `mprotect` succeeding where the sub-page one fails; every 4 KiB slot of a
  protected host page becoming unwritable; correct per-thread attribution in
  the concurrent case.
- Negative controls: `probe --negative` makes the handler record a null
  faulting address instead of `si_addr`, the exact defect that would make a
  tracking scheme silently unsound. `src/check.py` must print `FAIL` on that
  output and `PASS` on the normal output; `run.sh` runs both and prints each
  verdict.
- Baselines: the single-thread fault round trip is the baseline for the
  two-thread case; the unmodified store loop is the baseline for the software
  bitmap, with its own run-to-run spread reported as the resolution of that
  comparison.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: first-touch commit faults mixed into tracking faults;
  VM-map fragmentation accumulated by earlier probes in the same process;
  the handler's own bookkeeping and `mprotect` calls being inside or outside
  the timed interval; P/E-core placement and thread migration; thermal drift;
  clock ramp making whichever microbenchmark runs second appear faster; the
  compiler eliding stores; the two concurrent threads not actually overlapping.
- Mitigations and residual risk: the region is fully touched before
  measurement; the timed interval is defined explicitly as the store itself,
  which necessarily includes the handler and its `mprotect`, and that definition
  is stated with the result rather than left implicit; the software-bitmap
  comparison discards two warm-up passes, alternates the order of the two
  variants across 8 repetitions, and reports the unmodified loop's own spread
  as the resolution of the differential; the concurrent threads spin on a shared
  gate before starting. Residual risk: fragmentation from earlier probes is
  neither reproduced nor controlled, so `mprotect` cost is a lower bound;
  placement is uncontrolled; and the fixed sequential page order is not a real
  guest write pattern. All three are stated as limitations.

## Sample plan

- Units, repetitions, randomization/order, warm-up, and power/precision target:
  the fault unit is one protected page written; 2000 units single-threaded and
  500 per thread in the two-thread case, in fixed ascending page order. The
  `mprotect` unit is one call; 5000 units alternating between two protections.
  The bitmap unit is one repetition of 2^20 stores; 8 repetitions per variant,
  with two discarded warm-up passes and alternating position so a clock-ramp
  effect cancels rather than biasing one variant. Order statistics
  (min, median, p99, max) are reported for every timing distribution rather
  than a mean alone, because the tail is the product-relevant quantity. The
  precision target for the bitmap comparison is explicit and falsifiable: the
  differential counts only if it exceeds the unmodified loop's run-to-run
  spread, and the record declares which case obtains.

## Exclusion and stop criteria

- Predeclared exclusions: a run at a revision other than the committed
  experiment revision is excluded; a run whose checker reports `FAIL` supports
  no claim; the `--negative` output never supports a claim; a bitmap
  differential inside the declared resolution is reported as unresolved and may
  not be quoted as a cost.
- Stop/failure conditions: if any protected write produces no fault, or any
  recorded address falls outside the region, the tracking mechanism is recorded
  as unsound on this host and the result is escalated before any cost figure is
  reported — an unsound mechanism's cost is irrelevant. If the sub-host-page
  `mprotect` is silently rounded, the granularity result is escalated to
  `Q-02-01` as a correctness hazard. If concurrent faults are misattributed,
  the two-thread result stops and returns to the method gate, since a
  misattribution could be either a platform property or a probe defect and the
  probe cannot distinguish them.

## Correctness status

- Correctness criteria and outcome: soundness of the tracking mechanism — one
  notification per modified page, naming that page — is the correctness
  criterion, checked by `src/check.py`; the outcome is pending the evidence
  run. This record asserts no guest correctness property; `Q-02-01` and
  `Q-02-02` own those.
- Open correctness risks: the probe writes one byte per page in ascending
  order, which cannot expose a race between a handler re-protecting a page and
  another thread writing it, nor a lost notification under a fault storm. A
  16 KiB host page covering four guest pages means any protection-based scheme
  reports modification of three pages that were not modified; that is a
  correctness-relevant imprecision for invalidation decisions, not just a cost,
  and is recorded here for `Q-02-01`. Writes performed by the emulator itself
  (loader, device model) bypass both mechanisms entirely and are not tested.

## Alternative explanations

- Alternatives considered: (1) faults are recorded because the handler runs,
  not because tracking works — distinguished by comparing recorded addresses
  against the written pages rather than counting faults alone; (2) the
  two-thread slowdown is thread-creation overhead — distinguished by the shared
  gate, which excludes creation from the timed region; (3) the sub-page
  rejection is an artifact of the chosen offset — distinguished by the
  host-page-aligned control at the same address; (4) the signal-class
  difference reflects the mapping's history rather than the fault class —
  distinguished by using two freshly created mappings; (5) the bitmap loop is
  faster because of the bitmap — rejected on its face, which is why the
  comparison reports its own resolution instead of a signed differential;
  (6) the round-trip figure is dominated by the handler's `mprotect` rather than
  by delivery — not distinguished by this probe, and stated as a limitation
  with the interval definition attached to the result.
- Evidence distinguishing them: each is separated by a recorded field, by a
  control, or by an explicit statement that the record does not settle it.

## Limitations

- Scope, applicability, and uncertainty limits: observed evidence will apply to
  `Mac16,10` (M4) / macOS 26.6.2 (`25G83`) / SDK 26.4 only; `P1` replication is
  owed before any platform-wide use. The probe measures 1 and 2 threads, not 4
  or 8, so scaling to the mandatory SMP topologies is not established. Its
  write pattern is synthetic and sequential; real guest patterns need the
  pinned corpus of `Q-05-03`. `mprotect` cost is measured on an unfragmented
  region and is therefore a lower bound. The fault round trip includes the
  handler's own `mprotect` by construction, so it is a mechanism cost rather
  than a delivery cost — delivery alone is `INV-01-06`. The software-bitmap
  differential is explicitly below the method's resolution and may not be
  quoted as a per-store cost. No guest runs, no page-table walking is
  performed, no self-modifying code is executed, and no invalidation of
  translated code is exercised; those compose only in `Q-01-12`. Nothing here
  selects a tracking mechanism, a granularity policy, or an architecture family.
