## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-07` — What timekeeping and asynchronous-event
  facilities exist on the supported hosts, at what resolution and latency, and
  can guest timer and interrupt delivery be built on them?
- Decision(s) affected: kill assumption `KA-05` (monotonic clock and bounded
  timer/cross-thread delivery sufficient for guest timer and IPI semantics);
  `KA-08` for the timer/APIC device class; the interrupt half of `KA-11`; the
  timer/interrupt mechanism space screened by `Q-01-02` into families `F1`–`F4`;
  and the prototype method of `Q-01-12`.
- Why this must be resolved now: every family in the register needs a guest
  tick, a guest TSC read, and a cross-vCPU interrupt. If the host cannot
  supply a cheap monotonic clock and a bounded wakeup and cross-thread
  delivery path, the timer/APIC device requirement fails for all of them and
  routes to project stop input 3. Nothing downstream — the correctness suite,
  the prototype, the performance method — can be designed before the numbers
  exist.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the guest requirement is a tick at up to 1 kHz per
  vCPU plus one-shot deadlines, at 2/4/8 vCPUs; the host is used through
  public API only; core placement is not directly requestable and QoS is the
  documented lever, so QoS arms sample placement rather than control it;
  measurement uses the same clock family it characterizes, so clock cost and
  resolution must be established before any latency figure is interpreted.
- Assumptions to challenge: that "a monotonic clock exists" is one property
  rather than six (monotonicity, sleep behaviour, rate scaling, cross-core
  coherence, resolution, read cost); that a requested wakeup interval is
  approximately the delivered interval; that leeway 0 disables deferral; that
  a signal is an adequate interrupt because it arrives at all — signal
  coalescing loses the per-vector count a guest APIC needs; that a median
  characterizes a mechanism whose tail is the thing an interrupt lives in.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: public macOS API provides (a) a monotonic clock that is cheap
  enough to back a high-frequency guest clock read and never moves backwards
  within or across threads, and (b) wakeup and cross-thread delivery paths
  whose latency distributions are bounded well enough to carry a 1 kHz guest
  tick and a cross-vCPU interrupt at user-interactive QoS.
- Falsifier(s): any observed backward clock step within or across threads; a
  median wakeup error at the 1 ms target at or above 250 µs for **every**
  mechanism at user-interactive QoS, `DISPATCH_TIMER_STRICT` included, and at
  every concurrent-thread count; an unbounded or unmeasurable round-trip p99
  for the IPI analogue; any lost cross-thread event; or a documented mechanism
  whose deferral has no public opt-out and no diagnostic — a condition the
  cited dispatch pages already answer for that mechanism, since they document
  `DISPATCH_TIMER_STRICT`, which is why the probe exercises it. Cost alone is
  not a falsifier: a mechanism that works but is expensive is a `Q-04-01`
  scaling input, and routing it into a feasibility kill would evade the
  `Q-04-02` maturity rule.
- Falsifier consequence, pre-committed before data (`REV-044` change 8): the
  wakeup-error condition above is stated identically here and under Exclusion
  and stop criteria, and it routes the same way in both. It is **not**
  automatically a `KA-05` failure. A miss routes, in order, to `Q-01-22`
  (whether a guest time-base policy other than host-monotonic-slaved absorbs
  the error), to `Q-01-21` (whether the miss is confined to a power or
  background-execution stratum), and then to a narrowed applicability
  statement naming the strata where it holds. It becomes a `KA-05` failure,
  and only then routes to project stop input 3, if no admissible mechanism
  meets the threshold at any accepted stratum *and* `Q-01-22` finds no
  admissible time-base policy that tolerates the observed error. This ordering
  is fixed now because the condition is live rather than hypothetical: the
  `REV-044` reviewer measured 1 ms-target medians of roughly 513–526 µs across
  all three then-existing mechanisms on this host, about 2× over the bar. That
  measurement is not evidence and supports no claim here; it is recorded as
  the reason the routing had to be fixed before the run rather than after it.
- Predicted outcomes: clock reads in the tens of nanoseconds with a step
  matching the 24 MHz-class timebase; zero inversions; a proportional wakeup
  overshoot that is modest at user-interactive QoS and large at background
  QoS, and materially smaller under `DISPATCH_TIMER_STRICT`; signal delivery
  to a spinning thread faster than delivery to a parked thread, because no
  wakeup is required; and pooled concurrent-thread error close to the
  single-thread figure up to 8 threads on a 10-core host.

## Method

The combined-probe procedure below is historical. Current staged methods follow
as M07-T2 (proposed) and M07-C1 (accepted); neither repairs historical missing raw.

- Procedure and versions: (1) claim-level source audit of the SDK headers,
  manual pages, and Apple documentation captured on 2026-09-03 with digests;
  (2) a single C probe, `experiments/INV-01-07/src/timers.c`, built with Apple
  clang 21.0.0 against `MacOSX26.4.sdk`, run once per mode. The probe
  characterizes clock resolution and read cost first, then cross-thread clock
  comparability, then wakeup latency, then cross-thread delivery, so that no
  latency figure is interpreted before its instrument is characterized.
  Arms and their ordering are fixed in the code and recorded in
  `experiments/INV-01-07/README.md`.
- Inputs and configuration: 2000 samples per wakeup and signal arm, and 2000
  per thread in the concurrent arm; 200 000 reads per clock for the granularity
  and monotonicity scan; 200 batches of 1000 reads for read cost; 4 threads ×
  200 000 timestamps for the cross-thread clock arm. Wakeup mechanisms
  `mach_wait_until`, `nanosleep`, a zero-leeway `dispatch_source` timer, and a
  `DISPATCH_TIMER_STRICT` `dispatch_source` timer, at targets 100 µs, 1 ms, and
  10 ms. Concurrent arm: `mach_wait_until` at 1 ms with 2, 4, and 8 threads.
  QoS arms user-interactive and background. Each wakeup arm additionally emits
  its own dispatch setup cost and a timebase-conversion containment control
  measured with `clock_gettime_nsec_np(CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW)`, which needs no
  caller-side timebase arithmetic. **Control bracket after
  `MAN-01-07-02`, accepted algebraically by `REV-135`:** every same-thread
  sample reads `m0_outer`, `c0`, `base_abs`, performs the wait, then reads
  `woke`, `c1`, `m1_outer`, in that order. It computes inner
  `abs_to_ns(woke-base_abs)`, independent `c1-c0`, and outer
  `abs_to_ns(m1_outer-m0_outer)`. Rule `I16a` requires
  `independent + slack >= inner` and `independent <= outer + slack` for every
  same-thread sample at both QoS classes. `slack` is six times the larger of
  `ceil(timebase.numer/timebase.denom)` and the recorded positive
  `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW` resolution; it must be no greater than 0.5% of the target.
  Rule `I16b` additionally requires at least one sample per same-thread arm
  whose `outer-inner` width is no greater than 0.5% of the target. Missing,
  noninteger, zero, inverted, or overflowing inputs fail rather than being
  skipped. The checker derives slack independently and requires it to equal
  the producer's emitted value. Dispatch diagnostics are reported but not
  enforced because `woke` is taken on another thread. Per-arm diagnostics are
  the sample count, derived slack, containment-violation count, largest signed
  lower residual `inner-(independent+slack)`, largest signed upper residual
  `independent-(outer+slack)`, tightest bracket width, and tight-width count.
  Scheduling inside either paired-read window can widen the bracket but cannot
  cause a false containment failure; the width rule prevents perpetually wide
  brackets from passing vacuously. The primary wakeup interval remains
  `woke-base_abs`, but the read placement differs from both failed artifacts,
  so no wakeup figure may be compared with them as strictly like-for-like.
  Each signal sample additionally records a round trip measured
  entirely on the sending thread's clock. Environment (`sw_vers`, `sysctl`,
  `clang --version`, the `xcrun` SDK path **and its resolved target plus the
  SHA-256 of each cited header**, `codesign -d --entitlements`, `uptime`,
  `pmset -g therm`, `pmset -g batt`) is captured before the run and
  `uptime`/`pmset -g therm` again after it, so thermal drift across the run is
  visible rather than assumed absent, and so a result reviewer can bind the
  binary to the sourced headers rather than to a symlink.
- Declared method assumptions (`REV-044` change 12): the `pthread_kill` arm's
  handler calls `mach_absolute_time()` and stores to `atomic_ullong` and
  `atomic_int`. The atomics are guarded — the probe asserts
  `atomic_is_lock_free` for both at startup and exits if either is false — but
  **no source in this inventory documents `mach_absolute_time` as
  async-signal-safe**, and none is cited. Its use in the handler is therefore a
  declared assumption and a stated limitation of `CLM-01-07-15` and
  `CLM-01-07-19`, not an established property. The handler is installed with
  `SA_RESTART` and without `SA_ONSTACK`, so it runs on the interrupted thread's
  own stack; both facts are recorded here because they bear on how this
  mechanism would compose with the `KA-04` fault handler under `Q-01-12`.
- Evidence labels assigned to planned claims: `CLM-01-07-01`–`CLM-01-07-07`
  and `CLM-01-07-09` are Source-backed now; `CLM-01-07-08`, `CLM-01-07-10`, and

## Controls

- Positive controls: the aligned, ordinary case for each mechanism — a
  100 µs, 1 ms, and 10 ms target on the same mechanism, so a mechanism that
  reports the same latency at all three targets is visibly not tracking the
  request. The `pthread_kill` arm has a parked-target counterpart in the
  condition-variable and semaphore arms, so "delivery to a running thread" is
  measured against "delivery to a parked thread" rather than in isolation.
- Negative controls: `--negative` credits every wakeup sample with a quarter
  of its target interval and negates every delivery latency. A deadline wait
  then appears to return before its deadline and delivery appears to precede
  its send, both physically impossible. `src/check.py` must report
  `RESULT: FAIL` and exit non-zero on that run; a checker that passes it has
  not been validated and the pre-execution review should reject the run.
- Baselines: none. This is a capability probe under the `Q-05-01` staging. It
  compares against no other system, and no number in it may enter a `Q-00-04`
  aggregate or a `Q-00-03` baseline comparison.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: background host load and other processes' timers, which
  change coalescing behaviour; DVFS ramp and thermal drift over a run of
  roughly 30 minutes; arm order, since the arms run in a fixed sequence;
  the measurement thread's own QoS, which is reset to user-interactive between
  arms; the `dispatch_source` arm's per-iteration source creation, which adds
  setup cost to its latency; the spinning target in the `pthread_kill`
  arm, which holds a core and therefore changes the scheduler's state; and, in
  the concurrent arm, contention between the timer threads themselves on a
  10-core host.
- Mitigations and residual risk: the environment capture brackets the run so
  load and thermal state are recorded rather than assumed; the QoS class is
  explicitly restored between arms; the `dispatch_source` setup cost is now
  measured and emitted per arm rather than only disclosed, so its numbers can
  be corrected instead of merely treated as an upper bound. Residual risk: arms are
  not randomized or interleaved, so a monotone drift across the run would be
  confounded with the arm order. This is a stated limitation; if the result
  gate finds a drift signature, the run is repeated in reverse arm order
  rather than reinterpreted.

### Second-correction method — accepted; implementation pending

Replace the point estimate with a containment bracket. For every same-thread
sample, take the start reads as
`m0_outer = mach_absolute_time(); c0 = CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW; base_abs =
mach_absolute_time()`, and the end reads as `woke = mach_absolute_time(); c1 =
CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW; m1_outer = mach_absolute_time()`. The primary wakeup
interval remains `woke - base_abs`: its start is immediately before the wait
and its end immediately after it. The control computes:

- inner bound: `abs_to_ns(woke - base_abs)`;
- independent interval: `c1 - c0`; and
- outer bound: `abs_to_ns(m1_outer - m0_outer)`.

With correct conversion and comparable monotonic clocks, the independent
interval must lie between the inner and outer bounds. A deschedule in either
paired-read window widens the bracket but cannot create a false containment
failure. This removes scheduler latency from the error threshold instead of
trying to estimate or excuse it.

The proposed integrity rule has two parts, both fixed before code changes:

1. `I16a`: every same-thread sample at both QoS classes must satisfy the
   containment relation, allowing only a quantization slack equal to six
   times the larger of the rounded-up mach tick and the recorded
   `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW` resolution. Six quanta conservatively cover the four
   interval endpoints plus integer-conversion rounding. On the host of record
   both resolutions are 42 ns, so the derived slack would be 252 ns; this
   number is illustrative, not a platform-wide constant. The exact checks are
   `c_elapsed + slack >= inner` and `c_elapsed <= outer + slack`; missing
   fields, a zero resolution, `inner > outer`, or arithmetic overflow are
   integrity failures rather than skipped samples.
2. `I16b`: every same-thread arm must contain at least one sample whose outer
   minus inner bracket width is no more than 0.5% of the target. This prevents
   a perpetually wide bracket from passing vacuously. The derived slack must
   itself be no more than 0.5% of the target; a coarser instrument invalidates
   that arm rather than granting a larger exemption. Code-gate fault injection
   must demonstrate that both +1% and -1% timebase-conversion errors fail the
   combined rule at every target, while injected scheduling gaps inside either
   paired-read window only widen the bracket and do not fail containment. A
   fixture in which all samples are wider than the 0.5% bound must fail
   `I16b`, so the non-vacuity branch is exercised directly.

Each arm must emit the derived slack, containment-violation count, largest
signed lower containment residual `inner - (independent + slack)`, largest
signed upper containment residual `independent - (outer + slack)`, tightest
bracket width, and count of samples meeting the tight-width rule. Positive
residuals are violations; zero is the accepted boundary. These are control
diagnostics, not timer-performance results. The checker must derive the slack
independently from the recorded timebase and clock resolution rather than
trust a producer-emitted tolerance.

Dispatch arms remain reported but not enforced because their wake timestamp
is taken on another thread. Unlike the failed correction, the containment
rule can cover both user-interactive and background same-thread arms: an
arbitrary scheduling gap affects bracket width, not containment. The exact
formula, the six-quanta rounding budget, the 0.5% non-vacuity bound, and the
±1% injection requirement were accepted algebraically by independent
`REV-135`; independent `REV-139` accepted the reconciled full packet after the
`REV-136`–`REV-138` returns. This acceptance opens implementation only. The
amended code remains ineligible for evidence execution until validation and an
independent code return pass.

The reordered start bracket moves `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW` back before `base_abs`
and adds an outer mach read; the end bracket adds one mach read after `c1`.
The new `base_abs`/`woke` inner pair deliberately restores the primary
wakeup-measurement placement used before the first correction, while the outer
reads exist only for the control. A future run is therefore not strictly
like-for-like with either failed artifact, and no reviewer may compare their
wakeup figures as though the measured path were identical.
