## Question and decision relevance

- Question ID and text: `Q-01-07` — What timekeeping and asynchronous-event
  facilities exist on the supported hosts, at what resolution and latency, and
  can guest timer and interrupt delivery be built on them?
- Decision(s) affected: kill assumption `KA-05` (monotonic clock and bounded
  timer/cross-thread delivery sufficient for guest timer and IPI semantics);
  `KA-08` for the timer/APIC device class; the interrupt half of `KA-11`; the
  timer/interrupt mechanism space screened by `Q-01-02` into families `F1`–`F4`;
  and the prototype method of `Q-01-12`.
- Why this must be resolved now: every family in the register needs a guest
  tick, a guest TSC read, and a cross-vCPU interrupt. If the host cannot
  supply a cheap monotonic clock and a bounded wakeup and cross-thread
  delivery path, the timer/APIC device requirement fails for all of them and
  routes to project stop input 3. Nothing downstream — the correctness suite,
  the prototype, the performance method — can be designed before the numbers
  exist.

## Assumptions

- Declared assumptions: the guest requirement is a tick at up to 1 kHz per
  vCPU plus one-shot deadlines, at 2/4/8 vCPUs; the host is used through
  public API only; core placement is not directly requestable and QoS is the
  documented lever, so QoS arms sample placement rather than control it;
  measurement uses the same clock family it characterizes, so clock cost and
  resolution must be established before any latency figure is interpreted.
- Assumptions to challenge: that "a monotonic clock exists" is one property
  rather than six (monotonicity, sleep behaviour, rate scaling, cross-core
  coherence, resolution, read cost); that a requested wakeup interval is
  approximately the delivered interval; that leeway 0 disables deferral; that
  a signal is an adequate interrupt because it arrives at all — signal
  coalescing loses the per-vector count a guest APIC needs; that a median
  characterizes a mechanism whose tail is the thing an interrupt lives in.

## Hypothesis and falsifier

- Hypothesis: public macOS API provides (a) a monotonic clock that is cheap
  enough to back a high-frequency guest clock read and never moves backwards
  within or across threads, and (b) wakeup and cross-thread delivery paths
  whose latency distributions are bounded well enough to carry a 1 kHz guest
  tick and a cross-vCPU interrupt at user-interactive QoS.
- Falsifier(s): any observed backward clock step within or across threads; a
  median wakeup error at the 1 ms target at or above 250 µs for every
  mechanism at user-interactive QoS; an unbounded or unmeasurable p99 for the
  IPI analogue; any lost cross-thread event; or a documented mechanism whose
  deferral has no public opt-out and no diagnostic. Cost alone is not a
  falsifier: a mechanism that works but is expensive is a `Q-04-01` scaling
  input, and routing it into a feasibility kill would evade the `Q-04-02`
  maturity rule.
- Predicted outcomes: clock reads in the tens of nanoseconds with a step
  matching the 24 MHz-class timebase; zero inversions; a proportional wakeup
  overshoot that is modest at user-interactive QoS and large at background
  QoS; signal delivery to a spinning thread faster than delivery to a parked
  thread, because no wakeup is required.

## Method

- Procedure and versions: (1) claim-level source audit of the SDK headers,
  manual pages, and Apple documentation captured on 2026-09-03 with digests;
  (2) a single C probe, `experiments/INV-01-07/src/timers.c`, built with Apple
  clang 21.0.0 against `MacOSX26.4.sdk`, run once per mode. The probe
  characterizes clock resolution and read cost first, then cross-thread clock
  comparability, then wakeup latency, then cross-thread delivery, so that no
  latency figure is interpreted before its instrument is characterized.
  Arms and their ordering are fixed in the code and recorded in
  `experiments/INV-01-07/README.md`.
- Inputs and configuration: 2000 samples per wakeup and signal arm; 200 000
  reads per clock for the granularity and monotonicity scan; 200 batches of
  1000 reads for read cost; 4 threads × 200 000 timestamps for the
  cross-thread clock arm. Wakeup targets 100 µs, 1 ms, 10 ms. QoS arms
  user-interactive and background. Environment (`sw_vers`, `sysctl`,
  `clang --version`, SDK path and version, `codesign -d --entitlements`,
  `uptime`, `pmset -g therm`, `pmset -g batt`) is captured before the run and
  `uptime`/`pmset -g therm` again after it, so thermal drift across the run is
  visible rather than assumed absent.
- Evidence labels assigned to planned claims: `CLM-01-07-01`–`CLM-01-07-07`
  and `CLM-01-07-09` are Source-backed now; `CLM-01-07-08` and `CLM-01-07-10`
  are Inferred now; `CLM-01-07-11`–`CLM-01-07-16` are Hypotheses that become
  Observed only after the evidence run, its manifest, and the result gate.
  `CLM-01-07-17` stays a Hypothesis until `P1` replication.

## Controls

- Positive controls: the aligned, ordinary case for each mechanism — a
  100 µs, 1 ms, and 10 ms target on the same mechanism, so a mechanism that
  reports the same latency at all three targets is visibly not tracking the
  request. The `pthread_kill` arm has a parked-target counterpart in the
  condition-variable and semaphore arms, so "delivery to a running thread" is
  measured against "delivery to a parked thread" rather than in isolation.
- Negative controls: `--negative` credits every wakeup sample with a quarter
  of its target interval and negates every delivery latency. A deadline wait
  then appears to return before its deadline and delivery appears to precede
  its send, both physically impossible. `src/check.py` must report
  `RESULT: FAIL` and exit non-zero on that run; a checker that passes it has
  not been validated and the code gate should reject the run.
- Baselines: none. This is a capability probe under the `Q-05-01` staging. It
  compares against no other system, and no number in it may enter a `Q-00-04`
  aggregate or a `Q-00-03` baseline comparison.

## Confounders

- Known confounders: background host load and other processes' timers, which
  change coalescing behaviour; DVFS ramp and thermal drift over a run of
  roughly 30 minutes; arm order, since the arms run in a fixed sequence;
  the measurement thread's own QoS, which is reset to user-interactive between
  arms; the `dispatch_source` arm's per-iteration source creation, which adds
  setup cost to its latency; and the spinning target in the `pthread_kill`
  arm, which holds a core and therefore changes the scheduler's state.
- Mitigations and residual risk: the environment capture brackets the run so
  load and thermal state are recorded rather than assumed; the QoS class is
  explicitly restored between arms; the `dispatch_source` setup cost is
  disclosed here and means its numbers are an upper bound on that mechanism,
  not a fair figure for a long-lived timer source. Residual risk: arms are
  not randomized or interleaved, so a monotone drift across the run would be
  confounded with the arm order. This is a stated limitation; if the result
  gate finds a drift signature, the run is repeated in reverse arm order
  rather than reinterpreted.

## Sample plan

- Units, repetitions, randomization/order, warm-up, and power/precision
  target: the unit is one wakeup or one delivery. 2000 samples per arm, chosen
  so the reported p99 rests on at least 20 tail samples; 18 wakeup arms
  (3 mechanisms × 3 targets × 2 QoS) and 6 signal arms. Clock arms use 200 000
  reads for the granularity scan and 200 × 1000 reads for cost. Warm-up: the
  first arm of each mechanism absorbs first-touch and page-fault effects and
  is not excluded, which makes the reported figures conservative rather than
  cleaned. Order is fixed, not randomized — the confounder section records
  this as the residual risk and the remedy. One run per mode; no aggregation
  across runs is planned, because a capability probe needs a distribution from
  one recorded environment, not a mean across environments.

## Exclusion and stop criteria

- Predeclared exclusions: none. No sample is discarded. A late wakeup is data,
  not an outlier; a lost delivery is an integrity failure of the whole arm, not
  an excluded sample.
- Stop/failure conditions: the run stops and produces no claim if the build
  fails, if `NATEMU_RAW_ROOT` is unset or inside the repository, or if any
  integrity rule in `src/check.py` fails on the normal-mode run. A `--negative`
  run that the checker does not fail stops the code gate. A threshold miss
  (`T1`–`T4`) does not stop the run: it is a recorded finding about the host,
  routed to the result gate and, if it stands, to the `KA-05` disposition.

## Alternative explanations

- Alternatives considered: (a) an observed wakeup overshoot is timer
  coalescing; (b) it is scheduler delay in getting the woken thread onto a
  core; (c) it is the probe's own measurement overhead; (d) it is thermal or
  DVFS state. (e) For the QoS gap: it is core-type placement, or it is
  scheduling priority on the same core type.
- Evidence distinguishing them: (c) is bounded by the clock read-cost arm,
  which is measured first and is orders of magnitude smaller than any
  plausible wakeup error; (d) is addressed by the bracketing `pmset -g therm`
  capture; (a) versus (b) is *not* distinguished by this probe, and the record
  must say so — the three wakeup mechanisms differ in their documented
  coalescing exposure but share the scheduler, so a common overshoot across
  all three is consistent with either. (e) is not distinguished either,
  because `CLM-01-07-08` establishes that placement is not requestable; the
  QoS gap is reported as a QoS effect and never as a P-versus-E measurement.
